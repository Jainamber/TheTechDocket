## 1. Primary Source Discovery & Game Details

*   **Game Title:** *Term Sheet, Please* (stylized as *Term Sheet, Please*)
*   **Creator:** Bo Lau (Hacker News username: `bolau`)
*   **Launch Date:** November 11, 2026
*   **Platform & Tech Requirements:** Browser-based, point-and-click interface. Runs directly in the browser using JavaScript; no download or installation is required.
*   **Pricing:** Free (no pricing or monetization is stated on the page).
*   **Open Source Status:** Not stated as open source; no public repository is linked or mentioned.
*   **Core Premise & Setting:** 
    *   The game is a thematic, lo-fi parody/recreation of Lucas Pope’s acclaimed 2013 indie hit *Papers, Please*, replacing the dystopian border control setting with the fast-paced world of Silicon Valley venture capital.
    *   The game is set "one day after Demo Day".
    *   The player takes on the role of a venture capitalist/decision-maker evaluating Y Combinator (YC) startup founders who are raising a seed round.
*   **Gameplay Mechanics:**
    1.  **Receive a Case:** A case opens presenting a founder's file containing their pitch, records, and financials.
    2.  **Inspect Documents:** Players click on specific facts on the founder's papers to examine them and spot inconsistencies or discrepancies.
    3.  **Call & Interrogate Founders:** Players press "NEXT" to call the founder to their desk and question them about their records.
    4.  **Mail Notes:** Players can send follow-up mail notes at 17:00.
    5.  **Verify & Decide:** Players run a "VERIFY" step on the records and then log their final decision—**PASS**, **FUND**, or **HOLD**—by physically placing a decision sheet on the desk.
*   **Current Content Limits:** The game currently showcases only a single case: **Case 01**, featuring a founder named **"Elias Hart"** at 16:12.

### 2. Community Reception & Hacker News Controversy

The game reached the front page of Hacker News on November 11, 2026, garnering **213 points** and **85 comments**. The community discussion centered on several key themes:

#### A. The "Credit" Controversy (Lucas Pope / *Papers, Please*)
*   **The Issue:** The game launched without explicitly crediting Lucas Pope, the creator of the original 2013 indie hit *Papers, Please*.
*   **Community Reaction:**
    *   Commenter `smlavine` noted: *"Doesn't credit the original creator at all in the actual website?"*
    *   Commenter `impendia` urged: *"Bo, this looks very cool, but I'd encourage you to immediately add 'Based on Papers Please by Lucas Pope', with a link here: https://papersplea.se/"*.
    *   Some users defended the lack of credit under copyright law: *"Satire is protected by copyright law and does not require permission or attribution"*.
    *   Others argued that regardless of legal requirements, attribution is the ethical thing to do: *"Regardless of legality, I'd argue that it's the right thing to do in this case"*.
*   **Fact Tags:**
    *   **REPORTED_SECONDARY:** HN thread comments and community debate regarding copyright vs. ethical attribution.

#### B. Tone and AI-Cloning Debate
*   **The Issue:** Some users found the premise of cloning *Papers, Please*—a game with heavy, dystopian themes of totalitarian border control, poverty, and survival—and applying its mechanics to the high-stakes, privileged world of Silicon Valley VC funding to be somewhat "tasteless" or ironic.
*   **AI Element:** The discussion also touched on the ease of using LLMs to clone game mechanics and tone, leading to debates on whether AI-assisted generation constitutes true "creation" or mere rearrangement/plagiarism.
*   **Fact Tags:**
    *   **REPORTED_SECONDARY:** Sentiment analysis of the thread (25% positive, 30% mixed, 45% skeptical).

#### C. Technical and UI Feedback
*   **Acclimatisation Ramp:** Users complained that the game *"does too much too quickly"* with no learning curve.
*   **UI Constraints:** The examination area was criticized as too small, making multi-page documents hard to navigate. Some documents became buggy and could not be dragged back to the booth.
*   **Dialogue Boxes:** Text responses from founders were often too large for their designated dialogue boxes, cutting off text.
*   **Fact Tags:**
    *   **REPORTED_SECONDARY:** Specific UI complaints logged by user `x-complexity` and others on the HN thread.

### 3. The India Angle & Global Context (Quantitative Data)

While the game is a global parody of Silicon Valley's Y Combinator, it highlights the broader reality of YC's shifting relationship with international markets, particularly India.

#### A. YC's Shrinking India Footprint (The Macro Context)
*   **Total Portfolio:** YC has backed **213 India-linked startups** since its first local cohort in Summer 2012.
*   **The Series A Chasm:** Of those 213 startups, **only about 20** have successfully raised a Series A round or beyond—a conversion rate of **just ~9%**. (For comparison, top-tier early-stage strategies target a ~50% Series A progression rate).
*   **Batch Contraction:** YC's India cohort sizes have collapsed:
    *   **2021:** Peak of **65** Indian startups (or 64 in some records).
    *   **2022:** **43** Indian startups.
    *   **2024:** Just **4** Indian startups.
    *   **2025:** Only **1** Asian startup was welcomed: *Bolna* (an AI voice agent startup).
*   **The AI Pivot:** The decline is heavily attributed to YC's pivot toward AI services (over 70% of the 2023 intake was AI-focused). Unlike localized plays (food delivery, local fintech), AI startups compete globally from day one, reducing YC's appetite for India-first demographic plays.
*   **The Big Winners:** Despite low conversion rates, YC's India portfolio has produced four massive, highly valued unicorns preparing for domestic IPOs: **Razorpay** (W15 batch, valued at $7.5B), **Zepto** (W21 batch, valued at $7B), **Meesho** (2017 batch), and **Groww**.
*   **Blockbuster Exits:** In late 2025, YC raked in nearly **$200 million** from partial stake sales during the blockbuster Indian IPOs of **Groww** (November 2025, netting YC ~$117M with 10% remaining stake worth ~$1B) and **Meesho** (December 2025, netting YC ~$10M with ~$50M in remaining stock).
*   **Fact Tags:**
    *   **CONFIRMED_PRIMARY:** YC batch histories for Razorpay (W15) and Zepto (W21).
    *   **REPORTED_SECONDARY:** Tracxn data on India startup shutdowns (over 28,000 in 2023–2024), Mint reporting on YC's 9% Series A conversion rate, and Asia Tech Review data on YC's late-2025 IPO cash-outs.
