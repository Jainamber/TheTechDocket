* **Provisional Designation:** 2026 ED138.

    * **2016–2023:** Found in 44 Dark Energy Camera (DECam) detections across 14 nights, retrieved using the B612 Asteroid Institute's ADAM Precovery service.
    * **2019–2026:** Detected in five month-long observing sectors of NASA's Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS).

## 4. Why It Was Missed
* **Galactic Camouflage:** 2026 ED138 lies at a declination of approximately −43° and about 12° south of the Galactic mid-plane.
* **Milky Way Crowding:** For the last 25 to 30 years, it traveled through the part of the sky aligned with the dense core (bulge) of the Milky Way, where background starlight from millions of stars hides faint moving objects.
* **Southern Sky Deficit:** It lies in the southern sky, which historically has had far less survey coverage than the northern sky.
* **Deblending Failures:** Even in Rubin single-visit catalogs, it was omitted in 24 of 38 visits because the processing system combined nearby stars into regions too large for normal deblending.
* **Verification Status:** **CONFIRMED_PRIMARY** (Detailed in discovery paper and subsequent analysis).

## 5. Next Steps for Confirmation
* **The Dwarf Planet Criteria:** To be classified as a dwarf planet by the IAU, 2026 ED138 must:
  1. Orbit the Sun (Confirmed).
  2. Have sufficient mass for its self-gravity to overcome rigid body forces and assume a hydrostatic equilibrium (nearly round) shape (Unconfirmed).
  3. Not have cleared its orbital neighborhood (Confirmed).
  4. Not be a satellite of another planet (Confirmed).
* **Size Measurement Methods:**
  1. **Infrared Observations:** Measuring the thermal emission of absorbed sunlight to break the degeneracy between size and albedo (reflectivity). Planned observations target the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) and the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST).
  2. **Stellar Occultation:** Timing the duration of dimming as the object passes in front of a background star. Because it is currently crossing the dense star fields of the Milky Way, it is expected to generate numerous stellar occultations in the coming years, allowing precise shape and diameter mapping.
* **Verification Status:** **REPORTED_SECONDARY** (Expert commentary from Matthew Holman and Mario Jurić).

### 1. The Core Discovery: 2026 ED138
* **What it is:** A newly identified trans-Neptunian object (TNO) and a strong candidate to become the solar system's sixth recognized dwarf planet. 
* **Key Metrics & Orbital Mechanics:**
  * **Current Distance:** Approximately 46 Astronomical Units (AU) from the Sun (roughly 6.9 billion kilometers), compared to Pluto's average of 40 AU.
  * **Orbital Period:** Takes **281.1 years** (roughly 280 years) to complete one full orbit around the Sun.
  * **Estimated Size:** Between **600 and 1,500 kilometers** (370 to 930 miles) in diameter. The wide range is due to unknown surface reflectivity (albedo). If it has a low albedo (dark surface), it is closer to 1,500 km; if highly reflective, it is closer to 600 km.
  * **Orbital Tilt:** Inclined at nearly **25 degrees** (specifically ~24°) relative to the solar system's main orbital plane. This classifies it as a "hot classical" TNO, suggesting its orbit was disturbed by Neptune's gravity in the distant past.
* **Discovery Timeline:** 
  * **October 13, 2026:** First spotted by astrophysicist **Matthew Holman** (Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian) while running custom search algorithms on early data from the Vera C. Rubin Observatory.
  * **October 27, 2026:** Officially announced by the International Astronomical Union’s (IAU) Minor Planet Center (MPC) under provisional designation **2026 ED138**.
  * **Precovery:** Using the newly calculated orbit, scientists traced the object back in archival images from the Dark Energy Camera (DECam) to 2016, and even located it on photographic glass plates from December 1937 stored at Harvard.

### 2. The India Angle: Sourcing, Partnerships, and Data Rights
While the discovery of 2026 ED138 was made using the Vera C. Rubin Observatory in Chile (funded by the US NSF and DOE), India has a highly structured, formal connection to the observatory and the data that made this discovery possible.

#### A. The In-Kind Software Contribution & Data Access
* **The Data Rights Barrier:** Rubin Observatory’s Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) data is not an open public download. Access to the Rubin Science Platform is strictly restricted to US/Chilean institutions and international partners who hold official "data rights".
* **India's Entry Route:** In 2020, Indian astronomers secured official data rights by proposing an **in-kind contribution**. 
* **The Software Moat:** Instead of financial funding, Indian institutions contributed specialized software development. 
  * **Lead Indian Institutions:** The **Inter-University Centre for Astronomy and Astrophysics (IUCAA)** in Pune and the **National Centre for Radio Astrophysics (NCRA-TIFR)**.
  * **Principal Investigator (PI):** **Professor Yogesh Wadadekar** (NCRA-TIFR). He leads the directed software contribution where Indian postdocs and researchers build the processing software required for LSST’s science collaborations.
  * **The 60-Hour Rule:** In exchange for this software contribution, participating Indian astronomers gain access to proprietary Rubin Observatory images **60 hours after they are captured**. This data access is shared between the PI and up to four junior associates.

#### B. Key Indian Scientists Involved in the Rubin Collaboration
* **Prof. Surhud Shrikant More (IUCAA, Pune):** Serves as the **Rubin IUCAA Programme Manager**. He coordinates India's scientific utilization of the LSST data. 
  * *Note:* Prof. More was awarded India's prestigious **Vigyan Yuva-Shanti Swarup Bhatnagar Award 2025** (under the Rashtriya Vigyan Puraskar) for his exceptional contributions to cosmology, dark matter, and his leadership role in the upcoming LSST mission.
* **Prof. Yogesh Wadadekar (NCRA-TIFR):** Principal Investigator of India's software contribution to the Rubin Observatory.

#### C. Academic & Civil Services Focus in India
The discovery of 2026 ED138 has been heavily integrated into Indian educational and competitive exam ecosystems:
* It is a major current affairs topic for the **UPSC Civil Services Examination** (specifically under **GS Paper III: Science & Technology / Space Technology**).
* Leading Indian IAS prep portals (such as *Drishti IAS*, *IAS Parliament*, *GKToday*, and *Shanmugam IAS Academy*) published detailed breakdowns of the discovery, using it to teach the IAU's 2006 definition of a dwarf planet and the geography of the Kuiper Belt.
