## 2. Precise holdings

### Fair dealing / Section 52(1)(a), Copyright Act 1957
- Court held that OpenAI's **storage of ANI's works to train the LLM underlying ChatGPT** falls within the fair dealing exception at **Section 52(1)(a)** of the Copyright Act, 1957, and therefore does **not** amount to infringement under **Section 51**.
- Quote (as reported by Bar and Bench and LiveLawBiz, worded almost identically, indicating both are quoting the same order text): *"OpenAI's act of storing ANI's original literary works for training the LLMs underlying ChatGPT falls under Section 52(1)(a)"* and therefore *"does not amount to infringement under Section 51."* — [Bar and Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/openai-did-not-violate-copyright-laws-by-using-ani-news-to-train-chatgpt-delhi-high-court-declines-interim-relief), [LiveLawBiz](https://www.livelawbiz.com/copyright/openais-use-of-ani-content-to-train-chatgpt-is-not-prima-facie-copyright-infringement-delhi-high-court-542738). Cross-verified (2+ near-identical quotations, consistent with [SpicyIP](https://spicyip.com/2026/07/training-chatgpt-on-copyrighted-works-prima-facie-protected-under-indian-copyright-act-delhi-high-court-refuses-interim-injunction-against-openai.html) and [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/india/delhi-hc-refuses-to-restrain-openai-says-chatgpt-training-on-ani-content-not-prima-facie-copyright-infringement)).
- Note: Section 52(1)(a) covers fair dealing for purposes including private/personal use, research, criticism, review, and reporting of current events; multiple outlets describe the court's reasoning as resting on the "research" limb of this fair-dealing provision.

### Training vs. storage vs. output
- **Storage/training:** Treated as protected fair dealing (see above) — the act of storing/ingesting copyrighted text for model training is the specific act found non-infringing.
- **Output generation (RAG):** The court found that ChatGPT's responses — generated via **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)** — do not amount to infringement under Section 51 because they are **not substantially similar** to ANI's original works. Quote: *"ANI has failed to satisfy this Court that any memorisation or regurgitation of ANI's copyrighted literary works has happened to the responses generated by ChatGPT."* — reported near-identically by [Bar and Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/openai-did-not-violate-copyright-laws-by-using-ani-news-to-train-chatgpt-delhi-high-court-declines-interim-relief), [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/delhi-high-court/delhi-high-court-openai-ani-copyright-infringement-interim-relief-chatgpt-542746), [LiveLawBiz](https://www.livelawbiz.com/copyright/openais-use-of-ani-content-to-train-chatgpt-is-not-prima-facie-copyright-infringement-delhi-high-court-542738), [SpicyIP](https://spicyip.com/2026/07/training-chatgpt-on-copyrighted-works-prima-facie-protected-under-indian-copyright-act-delhi-high-court-refuses-interim-injunction-against-openai.html), [Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-ani-openai-copyright-dispute-delhi-high-court-interim-relief/). Cross-verified (5 sources, consistent wording — very likely a direct order quotation).
- Additional quote on storage purpose: *"storage by OpenAI is only for the purposes of training OpenAI's LLMs"* with the court finding no evidence of market substitution or subscriber loss to ANI — [Adgully](https://www.adgully.com/post/18456/delhi-hc-denies-interim-injunction-to-ani-in-openai-copyright-suit) [single-source].
- **Four issues framed by the Court** (per [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/deeptech/openai-vs-ani-copyright-infringement-case-delhi-high-court-judgement-llm-training-data-usage)):
  1. Whether storage of ANI content for training ChatGPT amounts to infringement
  2. Whether using copyrighted data to generate responses constitutes infringement
  3. Whether the use falls under Section 52 "fair dealing"
  4. Whether the court has jurisdiction

### Jurisdiction / territoriality
- **This is the one point where source coverage varies in depth, though not in substance where addressed.** OpenAI had challenged the maintainability of the suit before Delhi High Court, arguing its models were not trained in India and its servers are US-based.
- Sources affirmatively reporting the Court **rejected OpenAI's jurisdictional challenge** and held the suit maintainable / Delhi courts have territorial jurisdiction: [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/delhi-hc-says-openais-chatgpt-training-use-is-fair-dealing-denies-ani-relief-105487.htm) ("Delhi courts possess territorial jurisdiction despite OpenAI's US-based servers"), [SpicyIP](https://spicyip.com/2026/07/training-chatgpt-on-copyrighted-works-prima-facie-protected-under-indian-copyright-act-delhi-high-court-refuses-interim-injunction-against-openai.html) ("suit was maintainable... despite OpenAI's U.S. servers"), [Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-ani-openai-copyright-dispute-delhi-high-court-interim-relief/) ("ruled in ANI's favor on territorial jurisdiction"), [Adgully](https://www.adgully.com/post/18456/delhi-hc-denies-interim-injunction-to-ani-in-openai-copyright-suit) ("ruled in ANI's favor... allowing the suit to proceed"), [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/deeptech/openai-vs-ani-copyright-infringement-case-delhi-high-court-judgement-llm-training-data-usage) ("Indian territorial jurisdiction confirmed; OpenAI's maintainability objections rejected"). This is **cross-verified across 5 independent outlets**.
- Two sources ([Bar and Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/openai-did-not-violate-copyright-laws-by-using-ani-news-to-train-chatgpt-delhi-high-court-declines-interim-relief) and [LiveLawBiz](https://www.livelawbiz.com/copyright/openais-use-of-ani-content-to-train-chatgpt-is-not-prima-facie-copyright-infringement-delhi-high-court-542738)) note the jurisdiction issue was framed/argued but don't specify the court's finding in the excerpt captured — this reads as incomplete coverage in those particular articles rather than a substantive contradiction.
- **Background context:** in earlier 2025 hearings, amicus curiae Dr. Arul George Scaria had argued the High Court possessed jurisdiction — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/delhi-high-court/delhi-high-court-rejects-ani-copyright-suit-open-ai-chatgpt-542736).
- **Verdict on this fact: jurisdiction was decided in favour of maintainability / ANI, treat as verified (5-source cross-check).**

### Findings on ANI's specific claims
- **Verbatim reproduction:** Court found ANI failed to demonstrate that ChatGPT "memorised or regurgitated" its copyrighted works; outputs held not substantially similar to ANI's original content. (See quotes above; cross-verified 5+ sources.)
- **Hallucinated attribution:** ANI's claim that ChatGPT fabricated stories/quotes falsely attributed to it (including a specific instance cited in the original 2024 complaint — a fabricated interview/conversation with Congress leader Rahul Gandhi wrongly attributed to ANI — per [TechCrunch's original Nov 2024 filing report](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/18/indian-news-agency-sues-openai-alleging-copyright-infringement)) — was **noted but not treated as a ground for copyright injunction**: [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/delhi-high-court/delhi-high-court-rejects-ani-copyright-suit-open-ai-chatgpt-542736) reports "'Hallucinations' (false attributions) noted but not grounds for injunction" [single-source on this precise framing, but consistent with the general holding that no copyright infringement was made out]. This suggests the Court treated the hallucination/misattribution complaint as more of a reputational/defamation-adjacent grievance falling outside the copyright infringement analysis, rather than ruling on it as a separate cause of action.

## 3. Relief granted/denied; main suit status; next hearing

- **Relief: DENIED.** Interim injunction application dismissed. OpenAI is **not** restrained from using ANI's published content during the pendency of the suit. Confirmed universally across all sources fetched.
- ANI had sought: (a) interim injunction restraining OpenAI from using ANI material, and (b) a directive to delete/disable ANI works from training datasets — both denied — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/openai-use-of-ani-data-doesn-t-amount-to-infringement-says-delhi-hc-126072400913_1.html), [Adgully](https://www.adgully.com/post/18456/delhi-hc-denies-interim-injunction-to-ani-in-openai-copyright-suit).
- **Court's reasoning for denial — balance of convenience / public interest:**
  - *"ANI has failed to make out a prima facie case for grant of interim injunction. Balance of convenience is also against grant of interim injunction."* — [Bar and Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/openai-did-not-violate-copyright-laws-by-using-ani-news-to-train-chatgpt-delhi-high-court-declines-interim-relief), [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/deeptech/openai-vs-ani-copyright-infringement-case-delhi-high-court-judgement-llm-training-data-usage) (cross-verified, near-identical quotation).
  - *"Irreparable injury would be caused not only to OpenAI but to public interest also"* / *"irreparable injury would be caused not only to the defendant but also to the wider public interest"* — [Bar and Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/openai-did-not-violate-copyright-laws-by-using-ani-news-to-train-chatgpt-delhi-high-court-declines-interim-relief), [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/delhi-high-court/delhi-high-court-rejects-ani-copyright-suit-open-ai-chatgpt-542736), [Adgully](https://www.adgully.com/post/18456/delhi-hc-denies-interim-injunction-to-ani-in-openai-copyright-suit). Cross-verified (3+ sources).
  - On AI development/economics: *"It would be economically unviable to develop an LLM if training of an LLM would require licenses from multiple sources."* — [Bar and Bench (DPIIT white paper piece)](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/injunction-against-chatgpt-would-hurt-indian-ai-development-users-delhi-hc-differs-with-dpiit-white-paper) [single-source, but a direct order quotation as reported].
  - *"Any interim injunction granted at this stage would, in my opinion, be detrimental to the growth of AI and more particularly, to the LLMs being developed in India."* — same source [single-source].
  - *"It would also have adverse impact on public interest, including millions of users of ChatGPT in India, many of whom would not be paid subscribers."* — same source [single-source].
  - Notably, this Bar and Bench piece reports the ruling **explicitly diverges from a DPIIT (Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade) white paper/committee position**, which had proposed a mandatory blanket-licensing-with-statutory-royalty regime for AI training rather than resolving the fair-dealing question outright.
- **Main suit:** Continues. This was only a ruling on the interim injunction application; the substantive infringement suit proceeds to trial/further hearings.
- **Next hearing date:** **Not reported in any source fetched.** Multiple outlets explicitly note this ("Next Hearing: Not specified," "detailed order copy awaited" — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/deeptech/openai-vs-ani-copyright-infringement-case-delhi-high-court-judgement-llm-training-data-usage)). ## 5. Case background

- **Filing:** ANI Media Pvt Ltd filed the suit at Delhi High Court. Most sources say simply **"November 2024."** Two sources give more precise (and slightly conflicting) dates: [TechCrunch's original report](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/18/indian-news-agency-sues-openai-alleging-copyright-infringement) says filed **Monday, November 18, 2024**, with first hearing the next day; the [CMS Law case tracker](https://cms.law/en/int/publication/artificial-intelligence-and-copyright-case-tracker/ani-media-pvt-ltd-v.-openai-opco-llc) says **19 November 2024**. [Flag: single-day discrepancy, likely institution date vs. first-listing date — treat "November 2024" as the safe, well-verified fact, and note the 18th/19th split as unresolved single-source-each detail.] This was reported as **India's first generative-AI copyright infringement suit**.
- **Pre-suit history:** ANI had reportedly offered OpenAI a licensing arrangement in **October 2024**, which OpenAI declined, before ANI sued — [The Leaflet](https://theleaflet.in/digital-rights/ani-v-openai-in-the-delhi-hc-everything-so-far-and-all-that-is-at-stake) [single-source]. Separately, per the [Bar and Bench viewpoint piece](https://www.barandbench.com/view-point/ani-v-openai-is-a-copyright-case-its-also-a-masterclass-in-how-mandates-are-lost), ANI first asked OpenAI to stop using its content in **July 2024**, months before the suit was filed [single-source]. OpenAI **voluntarily blocklisted ANI's website (aninews.in)** from future crawling/training in **October 2024**, before the court hearings began — [LiveLawBiz](https://www.livelawbiz.com/copyright/openais-use-of-ani-content-to-train-chatgpt-is-not-prima-facie-copyright-infringement-delhi-high-court-542738) [single-source, but plausible/consistent with the "opt-out blocklisting" defense OpenAI raised generally].
- **Damages claimed:** **₹2 crore** (roughly USD 240,000 as reported) — per [Adgully](https://www.adgully.com/post/18456/delhi-hc-denies-interim-injunction-to-ani-in-openai-copyright-suit) and [The Leaflet](https://theleaflet.in/digital-rights/ani-v-openai-in-the-delhi-hc-everything-so-far-and-all-that-is-at-stake). **Cross-verified (2 sources)**, consistent with the figure widely reported at the time of filing in late 2024 coverage. ANI's suit also sought a permanent injunction and directions to disable/delete ANI content from training data.
- **ANI's core allegations:**
  1. OpenAI used ANI's copyrighted news content without authorization/licence to train ChatGPT.
  2. ChatGPT outputs allegedly contained verbatim/near-verbatim excerpts of ANI news articles.
  3. ChatGPT "hallucinated" — fabricating content/quotes and falsely attributing them to ANI, including (per the original complaint) a fabricated interview/statement wrongly attributed to ANI involving Congress leader Rahul Gandhi — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/18/indian-news-agency-sues-openai-alleging-copyright-infringement) [single-source for this specific example, though the general "hallucinated attribution" claim is corroborated across many sources].
  4. ANI's counsel argued such hallucinations risk public disorder given ANI's stature as a wire service — [TechCrunch] [single-source].
- **OpenAI's defenses:**
  1. Facts/news are not copyrightable; copyright protects expression, not underlying facts.
  2. Its process is transformative — ChatGPT generates responses "afresh" via statistical/linguistic pattern learning, not by storing or reproducing specific articles.
  3. Publishers can opt out via a blocklist/robots.txt-style mechanism; OpenAI said it does not need affirmative permission to use publicly available internet data.
  4. **Jurisdictional objection:** OpenAI has no servers in India and its models were not trained in India, so Delhi High Court lacks jurisdiction (this was considered and — per majority of sources — rejected; see Section 2 above).
  - Sources: [Bar and Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/openai-tells-delhi-high-court-it-lacks-jurisdiction-to-hear-ani-copyright-suit), [LiveLawBiz](https://www.livelawbiz.com/copyright/openais-use-of-ani-content-to-train-chatgpt-is-not-prima-facie-copyright-infringement-delhi-high-court-542738), [The Leaflet](https://theleaflet.in/digital-rights/ani-v-openai-in-the-delhi-hc-everything-so-far-and-all-that-is-at-stake), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/18/indian-news-agency-sues-openai-alleging-copyright-infringement).
