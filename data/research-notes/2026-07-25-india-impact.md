# Research Notes: India-Specific Stakes & Aftermath of Delhi HC's ANI v. OpenAI Ruling (July 24, 2026)

Compiled 2026-07-25. Topic: the July 24, 2026 Delhi High Court ruling denying ANI's interim injunction against OpenAI (ChatGPT training on ANI content held prima facie "fair dealing"), and what it means for Indian news publishers, Indian AI companies, and India's copyright/AI policy landscape.

Method note: WebSearch first, then WebFetch on URLs surfaced in results. Most direct court-order text was not fetchable; findings rely on legal-news outlets (Bar & Bench, LiveLaw, Medianama, LawChakra) and business press (Business Standard, Storyboard18, Outlook Business) reporting on the same order, cross-checked against each other. Marked **[single-source]** where only one outlet had a fact.

## 0. THE RULING ITSELF — core facts (heavily corroborated, 5+ sources)

- **Case:** ANI Media Pvt Ltd v. OpenAI Inc (and OpenAI OpCo LLC), CS(COMM) 1028/2024, Delhi High Court. Filed November 2024 — India's first generative-AI copyright infringement suit against an AI company.
  — SOURCE: https://lawchakra.in/high-court/ani-delhi-hc-openai-unfair/ ; https://www.malaymail.com/news/tech-gadgets/2024/11/20/indian-news-agency-ani-joins-global-lawsuit-wave-against-chatgpt-creator-openai-over-unauthorised-use-of-news-content/157446
- **Judge:** Justice Amit Bansal, Delhi High Court. Judgment on the interim injunction application was reserved March 27, 2026 and delivered **July 24, 2026**.
  — SOURCE: https://www.outlookbusiness.com/deeptech/openai-vs-ani-copyright-infringement-case-delhi-high-court-judgement-llm-training-data-usage ; https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/openai-did-not-violate-copyright-laws-by-using-ani-news-to-train-chatgpt-delhi-high-court-declines-interim-relief
- **Outcome:** Interim injunction **denied**. ANI failed to establish a prima facie case warranting emergency relief; the main suit continues to trial.
  — SOURCE: https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-ani-openai-copyright-dispute-delhi-high-court-interim-relief/ ; https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/openai-use-of-ani-data-doesn-t-amount-to-infringement-says-delhi-hc-126072400913_1.html ; https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/delhi-high-court/delhi-high-court-rejects-ani-copyright-suit-open-ai-chatgpt-542736
- **Core legal reasoning (fair dealing):** The court held that OpenAI's act of storing/using ANI's works to train the LLM falls under **Section 52(1)(a) of the Copyright Act, 1957** (the fair dealing exception), and therefore does not amount to infringement under Section 51.
  — SOURCE: https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-ani-openai-copyright-dispute-delhi-high-court-interim-relief/ ; https://www.outlookbusiness.com/deeptech/openai-vs-ani-copyright-infringement-case-delhi-high-court-judgement-llm-training-data-usage ; https://www.opindia.com/2026/07/delhi-high-court-reject-ani-interim-injunction-openai-copyright-infringement-case/
- **On output similarity / RAG:** Court found ChatGPT outputs (generated via Retrieval-Augmented Generation) were **not substantially similar** to ANI's original content, so outputs don't infringe under Section 51 either. Direct quote (repeated near-verbatim across outlets): *"ANI has failed to satisfy this Court that any memorisation or regurgitation of ANI's copyrighted literary works has happened [from responses generated by ChatGPT]."*
  — SOURCE: https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-ani-openai-copyright-dispute-delhi-high-court-interim-relief/ ; https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/openai-did-not-violate-copyright-laws-by-using-ani-news-to-train-chatgpt-delhi-high-court-declines-interim-relief ; https://www.opindia.com/2026/07/delhi-high-court-reject-ani-interim-injunction-openai-copyright-infringement-case/
- **On public interest (why interim relief was refused even if the merits were open):** *"Irreparable injury would be caused not only to OpenAI but also to the public at large if an interim injunction is granted."*
  — SOURCE: https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-ani-openai-copyright-dispute-delhi-high-court-interim-relief/ ; https://www.opindia.com/2026/07/delhi-high-court-reject-ani-interim-injunction-openai-copyright-infringement-case/ ; https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/delhi-high-court/delhi-high-court-rejects-ani-copyright-suit-open-ai-chatgpt-542736 (LiveLaw headline: "...Says It Would Harm Public Interest")
- **Other findings noted in reporting:** ANI presented no proof of lost market share/subscriber revenue; ANI's training data was described as a "minuscule amount" of OpenAI's total dataset; vector databases created during training were held not to constitute infringement by themselves; court found "no instance where OpenAI has used the literary works of ANI for any purposes other than for training."
  — SOURCE: https://www.opindia.com/2026/07/delhi-high-court-reject-ani-interim-injunction-openai-copyright-infringement-case/
- **Four issues framed for the case (from Nov 2024 framing order):** (1) whether storing ANI's data to train ChatGPT is infringement; (2) whether using that data to generate responses is infringement; (3) whether OpenAI's use qualifies as fair dealing under Section 52; (4) territorial jurisdiction. **Result at this stage:** issues (1) and (3) resolved in OpenAI's favor (fair dealing); issue (2) resolved in OpenAI's favor (no substantial similarity in outputs); **jurisdiction (issue 4) was decided in ANI's favor** — i.e., the Delhi High Court held it does have jurisdiction, so the case proceeds there.
  — SOURCE: https://www.outlookbusiness.com/deeptech/openai-vs-ani-copyright-infringement-case-delhi-high-court-judgement-llm-training-data-usage ; https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/openai-did-not-violate-copyright-laws-by-using-ani-news-to-train-chatgpt-delhi-high-court-declines-interim-relief
- **Counsel:** OpenAI represented by Senior Advocate Amit Sibal. ANI represented by Advocate Sidhanth Kumar. Amici curiae assisting the court: Dr. Arul George Scaria (NLS Bangalore professor, argued the court has jurisdiction) and Advocate Adarsh Ramanujan.
  — SOURCE: https://lawchakra.in/high-court/ani-delhi-hc-openai-unfair/ ; https://www.opindia.com/2026/07/delhi-high-court-reject-ani-interim-injunction-openai-copyright-infringement-case/
- **Wire pickup confirming this is a broadly-syndicated global story:** Reuters (Munsif Vengattil byline) reported the ruling; picked up by Moneycontrol, Financial Express, LiveLaw, Inc42, BestMediaInfo, Indian Express, and international outlets including The Star (Malaysia).
  — SOURCE: https://www.techmeme.com/260724/p12 ; https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/07/24/indian-court-rules-in-favor-of-openai-in-copyright-lawsuit-brought-by-news-agency-ani
- **OpenAI's position in the case:** Denies its systems store/reproduce articles verbatim; says outputs are "generated afresh rather than copied verbatim"; separately, OpenAI has argued **Indian courts lack jurisdiction** because servers are located outside India, and — regarding ANI's request that its content be deleted from training data — **OpenAI told the court that deleting the data would breach OpenAI's US legal obligations** (data retention/litigation-hold obligations in US proceedings, per reporting).
  — SOURCE: https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/openai-use-of-ani-data-doesn-t-amount-to-infringement-says-delhi-hc-126072400913_1.html ; https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/business/openai-confronts-copyright-dispute-with-indian-tycoons/story (headline referencing this Yahoo/Reuters exclusive; direct Yahoo fetch returned a 429 error and could not be independently re-verified in full — treat the "breach US legal obligations" framing as **reported but not independently re-confirmed by this research** beyond the headline/secondary reference)

## 1. INDIAN PUBLISHER COALITION POSITIONS

### Digital News Publishers Association (DNPA)
- DNPA is the umbrella body; per its own official "About Us" page, it represents **22 member publishers**: Lokmat, Indian Express, Zee Media, Dainik Jagran, Eenadu, ABP Group, India TV, Mathrubhumi, Malayala Manorama, India Today, The Hindu, Dainik Bhaskar, Network18, Times Internet, HT Digital (Hindustan Times), NDTV, The New Indian Express, The Mysore Printers, Amar Ujala, and Times Group. (Note: this is the full member list; individual news reports on the OpenAI litigation name only subsets of these as active intervenors — see below.)
  — SOURCE: https://www.dnpa.co.in/pages/about-us-7316203
- DNPA intervened in the ANI v. OpenAI suit, alleging OpenAI's scraping is **"large-scale, unauthorized, and illegal,"** that ChatGPT reproduces exclusive interview excerpts verbatim on prompting, that OpenAI ignores the robots.txt protocol, and that this shows **"dishonest intentions."**
  — SOURCE: https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/openais-failure-to-secure-licenses-in-india-shows-dishonest-intent-publishers-tell-delhi-hc-80418.htm
- DNPA specifically argued OpenAI's willingness to pay international outlets (Time, Financial Times, News Corp, Axel Springer) while refusing equivalent deals in India is itself evidence it knows licensing is required — i.e., a deliberate double standard.
  — SOURCE: https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/openais-failure-to-secure-licenses-in-india-shows-dishonest-intent-publishers-tell-delhi-hc-80418.htm
- DNPA warning quote: **"Print and digital news will vanish, only ChatGPT will remain,"** framing this as an existential threat to Indian journalism's economic model (traffic diversion, ad revenue decline, reduced media plurality, power concentrating with "a foreign entity").
  — SOURCE: https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/dnpa-raises-copyright-storm-in-delhi-hc-alleges-openai-is-erasing-news-media-146089.html
- Other intervenors supporting/alongside DNPA: **Federation of Indian Publishers**, the **IGAP Project**, and the **Broadband India Forum**.
  — SOURCE: https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/openais-failure-to-secure-licenses-in-india-shows-dishonest-intent-publishers-tell-delhi-hc-80418.htm

### Federation of Indian Publishers
- Joined the OpenAI copyright case (book/text publishers' apex body), part of a broader wave of intervention beyond news media into book publishing.
  — SOURCE: https://www.medianama.com/2025/01/223-federation-indian-publishers-openai/ ; https://www.techpolicy.press/hearing-in-copyright-case-against-openai-brought-by-indian-publishers-set-for-march/
- Separately, **global book publishers** (not just Indian) have also sued/intervened against OpenAI in the Indian proceedings.
  — SOURCE: https://finance.yahoo.com/news/global-book-publishers-sue-openai-123937217.html

### The Network18/NDTV/Indian Express/Hindustan Times intervention (Jan 2025)
- In January 2025, **Indian Express, Hindustan Times, Network18** (owned by Mukesh Ambani/Reliance), and **NDTV** (owned by Gautam Adani/Adani Group) filed a joint complaint in Delhi seeking to join ANI's existing suit — a separate, heavyweight intervention distinct from DNPA's institutional one, though DNPA (representing ~20 companies) also joined as a body. Their 135-page filing said OpenAI's scraping presents **"a clear and present danger to the valuable copyrights"** of DNPA members.
  — SOURCE: https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/business/openai-confronts-copyright-dispute-with-indian-tycoons/story
- **Notable defection:** despite being a DNPA member, **The Times of India opted OUT of this lawsuit/intervention.** Times of India is separately involved in a **Google** AI pilot program (see Section 2) — suggesting a split coalition where India's largest English-language publisher is pursuing a commercial-partnership track rather than litigation.
  — SOURCE: https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/business/openai-confronts-copyright-dispute-with-indian-tycoons/story

### Indian Music Industry (IMI) — Bollywood/music labels' intervention
- **IMI**, joined by major record labels **T-Series** and **Saregama India**, filed an intervention application in the ANI v. OpenAI case, extending the dispute beyond news media into the recorded-music/creative industries.
  — SOURCE: https://trademarklawyermagazine.com/indian-music-industry-files-intervention-application-in-ani-v-openai-copyright-case/ ; https://opentools.ai/news/indias-landmark-ai-copyright-case-expands-with-music-industrys-involvement
- IMI's core argument: unauthorized AI use of copyrighted works **"jeopardizes their business, which heavily relies on licensing revenue,"** and it separately challenged OpenAI's fair-use defense on the ground that training on copyrighted music directly harms rightsholders' economic interests, disrupting the law's intended balance (i.e., arguing against extending any "fair dealing" logic from news text to music).
  — SOURCE: https://trademarklawyermagazine.com/indian-music-industry-files-intervention-application-in-ani-v-openai-copyright-case/
- At the hearing, **Justice Bansal questioned whether IMI's intervention improperly expands the scope of the original ANI suit**, suggesting IMI might need to bring separate litigation; IMI's counsel argued for standing given direct case relevance to audio rights. (This suggests IMI's claims were not necessarily folded into the July 24 ruling's substantive findings — flagging as a scope question left partly open.)
  — SOURCE: https://trademarklawyermagazine.com/indian-music-industry-files-intervention-application-in-ani-v-openai-copyright-case/ **[single-source on this specific procedural exchange]**

### Crawler-blocking as a parallel/informal tactic
- Per DNPA figures cited in coverage, **over one-third (30%+) of major Indian news publishers have blocked OpenAI's crawlers** (GPTBot) via robots.txt, versus roughly two-thirds of leading news websites globally that had done so by late 2025. No individual Indian publisher was named in this specific figure. **[single-source]**
  — SOURCE: https://www.pitchonnet.com/pitch-feature/openai-access-blocked-by-over-of-top-indian-news-publishers-in-fair-share-clash-40224.html

## 2. LICENSING DEALS LANDSCAPE

### Indian publishers/agencies: zero AI-training licensing deals with OpenAI as of the ruling
- Multiple sources converge on this: **no Indian publisher or news agency has a training-data licensing deal with OpenAI** as of July 2026. Indian publishers' response so far has been litigation/intervention, not deals.
  — SOURCE: https://llmpulse.ai/blog/openai-publisher-deals/ ; https://pressgazette.co.uk/platforms/news-publisher-ai-deals-lawsuits-openai-google/ (confirms Indian Express, Hindustan Times, NDTV, ANI, DNPA are litigants, not licensors)

### The one partial exception: Times of India–Google (product pilot, not an OpenAI deal)
- The Times of India is one of **nine global publishers** in a **Google** pilot for AI-generated article summaries/"AI Overviews" in Google News (announced ~Dec 2025). The other eight: Der Spiegel (Germany), El País (Spain), Folha de S. Paulo (Brazil), Infobae (Argentina), Kompas (Indonesia), The Guardian (UK), The Washington Examiner (US), The Washington Post (US).
  — SOURCE: https://bestmediainfo.com/mediainfo/mediainfo-digital/google-pilots-ai-overviews-in-google-news-with-times-of-india-among-nine-global-partners-10900407
- Google describes this as a **"commercial partnership"** with compensation to participating publishers, sitting within Google's broader claim of **"paid content and display-rights partnerships" with 3,000+ publications globally**; specific dollar terms for the Times of India piece were not disclosed.
  — SOURCE: https://bestmediainfo.com/mediainfo/mediainfo-digital/google-pilots-ai-overviews-in-google-news-with-times-of-india-among-nine-global-partners-10900407
- Note: this is a **product/summarization pilot**, not confirmed to be an AI-model-training-data license — worth distinguishing from the News Corp/Axel Springer-style OpenAI training deals below when writing.

## 4. POLICY LANDSCAPE — MeitY/DPIIT and the Copyright Act question

- **DPIIT Working Paper on Generative AI and Copyright (Part I):** the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (under the Commerce Ministry) constituted a committee in 2025 — described in one source as including members from DPIIT, MeitY, NASSCOM, lawyers and academia, and in another source as **"a panel of eight experts"** (unclear if these are describing the same body or an inner core within a larger working group — **flagging as a minor discrepancy**, likely the same committee described at different levels of granularity). Working Paper Part I was **released December 8, 2025**, with public consultation open through **January 7, 2026**. Part II (addressing copyright ownership of AI-generated outputs) **remains pending** as of this research.
  — SOURCE: https://www.ikigailaw.com/article/655/exploring-the-dpiits-working-paper-on-generative-ai-and-copyright ; https://www.iam-media.com/guide/india-managing-the-ip-lifecycle/2026/article/the-future-of-indian-copyright-legislation-in-the-wake-of-ani-media-v-openai (panel of eight framing)
- **The paper explicitly cites ANI v. OpenAI as the proximate trigger** for the government's policy review — i.e., the litigation directly drove the policy process, and now the July 24 ruling will likely feed back into it.
  — SOURCE: https://www.ikigailaw.com/article/655/exploring-the-dpiits-working-paper-on-generative-ai-and-copyright
- **Recommended approach — the "Hybrid Model":** mandatory blanket licensing coupled with a statutory remuneration right (i.e., AI developers don't need prior consent from each rightsholder, but must pay). Key proposed institutional machinery:
  - A new government-designated collective-management body: **CRCAT — Copyright Regulatory and Compensation Authority for Training** — to administer centralized royalty allocation and dispute resolution, and to define the minimum revenue threshold that triggers royalty obligations (i.e., small/early-stage AI developers may be exempt until they cross a commercial threshold).
  - A **Rate Setting Committee** that would revisit/reset royalty rates **every three years**.
  - A flat **revenue-share model** pegged to a commercializing AI system's revenues.
  — SOURCE: https://www.ikigailaw.com/article/655/exploring-the-dpiits-working-paper-on-generative-ai-and-copyright ; https://www.iam-media.com/article/department-of-promotion-of-industry-and-internal-trade-committee-proposes-hybrid-model-address-ai-copyright-concerns ; https://www.intepat.com/blog/ai-training-copyright-india-dpiit-hybrid-model
- **MeitY's position:** MeitY has publicly **backed the DPIIT hybrid/mandatory-licensing framework**, explicitly rejecting both extremes — unrestricted free data access for AI training, and a pure individual-consent model. Quote attributed to MeitY's submission: *"Public good, while a worthy objective, cannot become a qualifying criterion for innovation, or unconditional immunity against legal challenges."* MeitY frames AI training as "value aggregation built on human creative labour" rather than a purely technical/exempt activity, and favors statutory remuneration at scale over case-by-case creator consent.
  — SOURCE: https://www.medianama.com/2025/12/223-meity-mandatory-ai-copyright-licensing-tech-industry-lawful-access/ ; https://www.varindia.com/news/meity-backs-dpiit-framework-on-copyright-use-for-ai-training
- **Tech industry opposition:** industry groups (named: the **Business Software Alliance**) argue developers should be free to train on lawfully accessed content, with copyright law intervening only if AI *outputs* infringe — characterizing training itself as non-copyrightable "pattern extraction," i.e., a full rejection of any mandatory-royalty framework.
  — SOURCE: https://www.medianama.com/2025/12/223-meity-mandatory-ai-copyright-licensing-tech-industry-lawful-access/
- **Unresolved/open questions flagged in the policy debate:** how to value different creators'/content types' contributions; the scope of CRCAT's pricing/distribution authority; whether statutory compensation genuinely substitutes for creator control (or just legalizes takings at a government-set price).
  — SOURCE: https://www.medianama.com/2025/12/223-meity-mandatory-ai-copyright-licensing-tech-industry-lawful-access/
- **Current state of the law (no amendment yet):** as of this research, **there is no proposal to amend the Copyright Act itself** or introduce a new statutory right specific to AI training — the Commerce Ministry panel's work is exploratory/consultative, not yet legislative. Recommended areas the panel is expected to examine: (1) a text-and-data-mining (TDM) exception modeled loosely on Japan's approach; (2) preserving creator incentives/licensing-fee mechanisms; (3) AI-output transparency/disclosure rules modeled on the EU AI Act; (4) collective-licensing or lump-sum remuneration schemes.
  — SOURCE: https://www.iam-media.com/guide/india-managing-the-ip-lifecycle/2026/article/the-future-of-indian-copyright-legislation-in-the-wake-of-ani-media-v-openai
- **Strategic/competitiveness framing in commentary:** analysts warn that an overly restrictive Indian framework could push AI development to more permissive jurisdictions, fragmenting the market and hurting India's AI competitiveness — a tension explicitly in play now that the July 24 ruling has landed on the permissive side (fair dealing) while DPIIT's own working paper leans toward mandatory paid licensing. These two tracks (judicial vs. executive/policy) are **not yet reconciled** and are worth flagging as a live contradiction: a court applying existing 1957/2012-vintage Section 52 case-by-case might reach different outcomes than a future statutory licensing regime the government is simultaneously designing.
  — SOURCE: https://www.iam-media.com/guide/india-managing-the-ip-lifecycle/2026/article/the-future-of-indian-copyright-legislation-in-the-wake-of-ani-media-v-openai
- **No government statement specifically reacting to the July 24 ruling** (as opposed to the pre-existing DPIIT/MeitY policy process) was found in this research.

## 6. WHAT HAPPENS NEXT IN THE CASE

- **This was an interim-injunction ruling only** — it does not decide the underlying suit. The main copyright infringement suit (ANI Media Pvt Ltd v. OpenAI, CS(COMM) 1028/2024) **continues to trial**; today's prima facie findings on fair dealing are not final and won't bind the eventual trial judgment.
  — SOURCE: https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-ani-openai-copyright-dispute-delhi-high-court-interim-relief/ ; https://www.opindia.com/2026/07/delhi-high-court-reject-ani-interim-injunction-openai-copyright-infringement-case/ ; https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/openai-use-of-ani-data-doesn-t-amount-to-infringement-says-delhi-hc-126072400913_1.html
- **Appeal to a Division Bench:** ANI is legally entitled to appeal Justice Bansal's single-judge interim order to a Division Bench of the Delhi High Court (standard procedure for interim orders in Indian civil litigation), but **no source found in this research confirms ANI has actually filed, or announced intent to file, such an appeal** as of the ruling date/immediately after. No direct ANI statement (spokesperson quote, press release) reacting to the ruling was located either.
  — Editorial note: treat "expected appeal" as **plausible-but-unconfirmed**; do not state as fact without a fresh, dated source closer to publication.
- A **detailed written order was reported as still pending** at the time several outlets published (i.e., the oral pronouncement/summary came first); full written reasoning may add detail not yet public.
  — SOURCE: https://www.opindia.com/2026/07/delhi-high-court-reject-ani-interim-injunction-openai-copyright-infringement-case/
- **Precedent value flagged by legal commentary:** this is described as the **first substantive Indian judicial finding** on whether AI companies can use copyrighted news content for training without a license — expected to be persuasive (though not strictly binding) in parallel/future Indian AI-copyright litigation, including the IMI music-industry claims and the Federation of Indian Publishers' book-publishing claims within the same case, plus any future suits by publishers who did not intervene.
  — SOURCE: https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-ani-openai-copyright-dispute-delhi-high-court-interim-relief/
- **Possible alternate resolution — settlement into a licensing deal:** the May 2026 Grupo Folha/Grupo UOL–OpenAI deal (which settled a pending 2025 copyright suit in Brazil) is a live template for how ANI's suit could eventually resolve outside of a full trial judgment, i.e., a negotiated license rather than a court decision. This is analyst-style inference by this researcher based on the Folha precedent, not something reported as being discussed for the ANI case specifically.
  — SOURCE: https://llmpulse.ai/blog/openai-publisher-deals/

### Explainer on Section 52 / fair dealing mechanics (useful for FAQ answer-writing)
- Section 52 of India's Copyright Act, 1957 (last substantively updated 2012) permits "fair dealing" for purposes including private study, research, criticism, and news reporting — but **was written before machine learning existed** and doesn't explicitly address computational/AI training use. This is the statutory gap the DPIIT working paper and this litigation are both grappling with.
  — SOURCE: https://www.mondaq.com/india/copyright/1786566/is-training-ai-on-copyrighted-content-an-infringement
- Notably, in its own DPIIT working-paper submission, the **government has separately signaled that "commercial AI training does not fall within the [existing] fair dealing exceptions"** — i.e., the executive branch's working assumption (favoring mandatory licensing) is in some tension with the judiciary's July 24 finding that OpenAI's training does qualify as Section 52 fair dealing. Worth foregrounding as the central "who's right" tension for readers.
  — SOURCE: https://www.mondaq.com/india/copyright/1786566/is-training-ai-on-copyrighted-content-an-infringement
