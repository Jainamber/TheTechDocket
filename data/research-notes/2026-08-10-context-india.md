## Regulatory context (fact — source — URL)

- **EU AI Act — Aug 2, 2026 is an enforcement-powers date, not a new-obligations date.** The European AI Office became formally entitled to investigate and enforce GPAI obligations on Aug 2, 2026. Substantive GPAI obligations (including systemic-risk duties: adversarial testing, incident reporting, cybersecurity) have technically applied since Aug 2, 2025 for models placed on the market from that date, but the Office lacked enforcement teeth until now; providers of models placed on the market before Aug 2, 2025 have until Aug 2, 2027 to comply. The Office can now compel technical documentation, run its own evaluations, require corrective measures, and fine up to €15M or 3% of global turnover, whichever is higher. — Wilson Sonsini; Beam.ai; Accuroai — https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/eu-ai-act-enforcement-phase-begins.html ; https://beam.ai/agentic-insights/eu-ai-act-enforcement-august-2-2026-gpai-fines ; https://accuroai.co/blog/eu-ai-act-what-actually-applies-august-2-2026

- **US — June 2, 2026 executive order takes a voluntary, non-regulatory approach.** President Trump signed "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security," which sets up a framework for early federal government access to frontier AI models — developers may voluntarily give government up to 30 days of early access before wider release. It creates no licensing regime or preclearance requirement. NIST is named as a consulted party (not the lead) in a benchmarking process led by Treasury, the Department of War/NSA, and DHS/CISA. Reported as emerging from internal administration debate between national-security concerns about AI-accelerated cyberattacks and a general deregulatory posture. — Skadden; Wiley; WilmerHale; Debevoise; Crowell & Moring — https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/06/new-ai-executive-order ; https://www.wiley.law/alert-New-AI-Executive-Order-Addresses-Frontier-Models-and-Cybersecurity-Vulnerabilities ; https://www.debevoisedatablog.com/2026/06/09/new-executive-order-targets-cyber-risks-from-frontier-ai/

- **UK AISI's mandate was narrowed to security/crime, not renamed cosmetically.** The UK AI Safety Institute was established Nov 2023 (announced at the Bletchley Park AI Safety Summit) under the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology. On Feb 14, 2025, Technology Secretary Peter Kyle announced at the Munich Security Conference that it would become the "AI Security Institute," dropping "Safety" from its name (formalized via a written statement to Parliament on Feb 24, 2025). The shift moved its focus from broad existential/alignment safety toward near-term national-security risks — cyberattacks, cyber fraud, CBRN, child exploitation — and included a new partnership with the Defence Science and Technology Laboratory (Dstl), the Ministry of Defence's science arm. This is the same body that published the Aug 4, 2026 unsanctioned-actions report above. — Infosecurity Magazine; UK Parliament written statement; Wikipedia (AI Security Institute) — https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/uk-ai-safety-institute-rebrands/ ; https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-statements/detail/2025-02-24/hlws454 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_Security_Institute

## India angle (fact — source — URL)

- **IndiaAI Safety Institute exists on paper and is mid-build, not confirmed fully operational.** India announced intent to set up an AI Safety Institute in January 2025 (ahead of co-hosting the Paris AI Action Summit), incubated under the IndiaAI Mission's "Safe & Trusted AI" pillar, using a virtual "hub-and-spoke" model (no single physical site) engaging academia, startups, industry, civil society and government. It was then formally proposed as an institution under the AI Governance Guidelines. As of a Digital India Corporation (under MeitY) job listing published May 19, 2026 (applications closed June 2, 2026), the Institute was **still hiring its Director** — the role that would oversee AI model evaluation, red-teaming and risk-assessment programmes. **I found no source confirming the Director role has since been filled or that the Institute is fully operational as of Aug 2026.** — Medianama (Jul 2026); IndiaAI.gov.in; iasgyan.in — https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-meity-india-ai-safety-institute-director-hiring/ ; https://indiaai.gov.in/news/meity-hosts-consultation-for-establishing-india-ai-safety-institute-under-indiaai-mission-s-safe-and-trusted-pillar ; https://www.iasgyan.in/daily-current-affairs/designing-indias-ai-safety-institute-aisi

- **Separately, the IndiaAI Mission's Safe & Trusted AI pillar has approved 13 projects** (as of Jul 2026) targeting deepfakes and bias — this is a pillar-level program result, not confirmation that the Safety Institute itself is staffed/running. — DD News/DD India — https://ddnews.gov.in/en/indiaai-missions-safe-trusted-ai-pillar-advances-responsible-ai-13-projects-approved-to-tackle-deepfakes-and-bias/

- **CERT-In has published AI-specific guidance, most recently May 25, 2026.** "Blueprint for Reducing Exposure and Defending against AI-Assisted Vulnerabilities Exploitation in Digital Infrastructure" addresses AI-accelerated reconnaissance, vulnerability discovery, exploit generation and phishing at scale; recommends an AI-governance framework (usage policies, approval/review mechanisms, AI-system inventories, shadow-AI detection) and a two-phase hardening plan (0–7 days: MFA, patching, baseline monitoring, workforce awareness; 8–30 days: SOC strengthening, telemetry integration, cloud/API assessments, tabletop exercises). — CERT-In (official PDF), Medianama, Lexology — https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/Blueprint_for_Defending_against_AI_Assisted_Exploitataion.pdf ; https://www.medianama.com/2026/05/223-cert-in-releases-blueprint-for-defending-against-ai-assisted-cyber-threats/

- **India's AI governance approach is voluntary/self-certification, layered on existing law rather than a standalone AI Act.** MeitY's AI Governance Guidelines set out seven governing principles and propose three new institutions, favoring voluntary compliance, self-certification and regulatory sandboxes over binding rules. The Guidelines state explicitly that the DPDP Act 2023, IT Act 2000, and sectoral rules (RBI/SEBI/IRDAI) already apply to AI systems — so an AI system processing personal data is already subject to DPDP consent, purpose-limitation, minimization, security-safeguard and breach-notification duties. Separately, MeitY notified IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Amendment Rules, 2026 on Feb 20, 2026, targeting "synthetically generated information" (AI-generated content designed to appear authentic). — Lexology; SARC Global; newsonair.gov.in — https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=ffc0c58c-3727-4472-9914-5fa6a33ffffd ; https://sarc.global/insights/meity-ai-governance-guidelines-india-2026 ; https://www.newsonair.gov.in/meity-unveils-india-ai-governance-guidelines-to-promote-safe-and-responsible-ai-adoption — **NOTE: I found a date conflict on when these Guidelines were released — see Unverified/conflicting.**

- **India's three largest IT services firms have scaled agentic AI deployment past 300,000 seats.** By June 2026, TCS, Infosys and Wipro had each scaled Microsoft 365 Copilot past 100,000 employees, a combined total exceeding 300,000 — up from a joint 50,000-seat commitment announced Dec 2025. Company-level detail: Wipro reports saving 250,000+ FTE-days per quarter and says its workforce has built more than 29,000 custom AI agents; TCS reports up to 35% reductions in selective work-cycle times; Infosys reports 91% monthly-active-user rate on deployed AI tooling. Separately, Infosys, TCS, Tech Mahindra and Wipro together are reported to be upskilling "nearly half a million" developers/consultants to build AI agents. — Microsoft News (Source Asia, official); PeopleMatters; Daily Pioneer; BW People — https://news.microsoft.com/source/asia/2026/06/03/infosys-tcs-and-wipro-scale-microsoft-365-copilot-to-over-300000-employees/ ; https://dailypioneer.com/news/indian-firms-move-from-ai-experimenting-to-core-integration ; https://www.peoplematters.in/news/ai-and-emerging-tech/infosys-tcs-and-wipro-now-have-300000-employees-using-copilot-as-ai-moves-into-daily-work-50090

- **CERT-In handled 29.44 lakh (~2.94 million) cyber incidents in 2025**, per its report released Jan 23, 2026, alongside 1,530 alerts and 390 vulnerability notes issued; 231 empanelled cybersecurity audit organizations; Cyber Swachhta Kendra covering 98% of India's digital population (1,427 organizations onboarded, 89.55 lakh malware-removal downloads); 122 cybersecurity drills conducted, including tabletop exercises for 1,570 organizations. ## Unverified / conflicting

- **India AI Governance Guidelines release date conflict.** One source (newsonair.gov.in, a government-linked outlet) dates the "MeitY unveils India AI Governance Guidelines" announcement to Nov 5, 2025. Another (SARC Global, law-firm summary) frames the Guidelines as "introduced" at the "India AI Impact Summit" in February 2026. Could not reconcile without a direct document fetch (blocked by network constraint). Plausible explanation — an initial Nov 2025 release followed by a higher-profile Feb 2026 Summit unveiling — but this is inference, not confirmed. Recommend spot-check before publishing a specific date.
- **Meta disclosure date off-by-one.** Washington Post and ETV Bharat date the Meta hack disclosure to Aug 6, 2026; CNN's article URL slug reads Aug 5, 2026. Used Aug 6 as primary (matches the higher-authority source and the task brief's framing) but flagging the discrepancy — could be a timezone/first-publish artifact rather than a real factual conflict.
- **CERT-In's category-level 2025 incident breakdown and 2024 YoY comparison are lower-confidence.** A first-pass synthesized search surfaced sub-figures (24.36 lakh "unauthorized scanning/probing" ≈ 83% of total; 3.41 lakh vulnerable services; 1.48 lakh malware; 8,386 defacements; 1,118 intrusions; 806 phishing; and a comparison to 20.41 lakh incidents in 2024, i.e. +44% YoY). A second, more targeted search re-confirmed the 29.44-lakh 2025 topline via 7 independent outlets but did not restate this category breakdown or the 2024/44% comparison. Treat the topline as solid; treat the sub-breakdown and YoY-growth figure as needing a direct-source spot-check before publication.
- **IndiaAI Safety Institute operational status is genuinely unclear**, not just under-reported — see India angle section above. Some coverage (DD News) reads as if the Institute is actively running programs; the most concrete, dated evidence found (a Director job posting open May 19–Jun 2, 2026) suggests it may still lack confirmed senior leadership. Both facts are individually sourced; the tension between them is real and should be surfaced rather than resolved one way in copy.
- **UK AISI's "Jul 25–28, 2026" incident date range** for the unsanctioned-actions report came from a synthesized search answer, not a verbatim quoted snippet from aisi.gov.uk itself. Moderate confidence; spot-check if the exact date range matters for the piece.
- **Model codenames "Mythos 5" (Anthropic/Claude) and "GPT-5.6 Sol" (OpenAI)** appear consistently across multiple independent outlets (Crowdfund Insider, Axios, Al Jazeera, Resultsense) so are treated as verified-by-repetition, but sit outside prior model-naming knowledge — double-check official capitalization/branding against the primary AISI report before publication.
- **"OpenAI June 2026 Report on Malicious Uses of AI"** — referenced by two lesser-known aggregators (tokenharbor.ai, aipulsen.com) as distinct from the confirmed Feb 25, 2026 OpenAI report; no direct openai.com URL found for a June-dated report. - **No Indian government/regulator reaction found** to the Aug 2026 disclosures or UK AISI report specifically (see India angle section) — confirmed absence across a direct, targeted search, consistent with a similar finding in this project's `2026-07-31-india-serp.md` note for the July incident.
