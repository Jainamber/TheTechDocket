## Discrepancies / Uncertain

1. **Terra's "current" price is inconsistent across OpenAI's own domains.** The official announcement blog (openai.com/index/advancing-the-price-performance-frontier-with-gpt-5-6/) states new Terra pricing of $2.00 input / $12.00 output. But OpenAI's own developer model-docs page — https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-5.6-terra — still shows the OLD pricing ($2.50 input / $0.25 cached / $15.00 output) as of this research (Aug 1, 2026, two days after the cut), confirmed on two separate fetches. This looks like a documentation-lag issue on OpenAI's own site rather than a real pricing conflict — the blog post, Sam Altman's quote, and every news outlet (InfoWorld, eWeek, MLQ, Axios, Forbes, third-party aggregator aipricing.guru dated July 31) agree on $2.00/$12.00 as current — but it means the "official" developer docs cannot be treated as authoritative for Terra's live price at time of writing.

2. **Exact verbatim wording of Sam Altman's X post is uncertain.** The primary source (x.com/sama/status/2082880720989532597) could not be fetched — blocked by robots.txt. Two secondary outlets (explainx.ai and StartupTalky) render the quote differently (see Verbatim Quotes above). The substance (Luna −80% to $0.20/$1.20; Terra −20% to $2/$12; Sol gets Fast mode) is consistent across both renderings and matches all numeric reporting, but the exact punctuation/wording is not independently confirmed.

3. **CNBC's article could not be directly fetched** (403 error on three attempts at https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/30/open-ai-price-cut-gpt.html). A Yahoo Finance syndication with matching headline/content was used instead (https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/openai-cuts-gpt-5-6-173045044.html); it corroborates the InfoWorld/eWeek numbers exactly. VentureBeat's article (venturebeat.com/technology/ai-price-wars-openai-cuts-gpt-5-6-luna-prices-by-80-as-model-competition-shifts-toward-cost) also returned a 403 both times it was fetched directly — its content is NOT included in this file beyond the headline seen in search results; do not attribute claims to VentureBeat.

4. **Which competitor actually drove the cut is disputed/unstated by OpenAI.** OpenAI's own blog frames the cut purely in efficiency terms and does not name a competitor. Press coverage disagrees on which rival model is the "real" driver: MLQ names DeepSeek V4 Pro (claiming it launched the same day and undercuts Luna on output pricing: $0.87 vs Luna's $1.20, though DeepSeek's input at $0.435 is described as higher than Luna's $0.20); StartupTalky names Kimi K3 (Moonshot AI, said to price at $3 input/$15 output); Cryptopolitan/Finout reference a broader basket (DeepSeek, Zhipu, Moonshot) capturing outsized OpenRouter token share (figures ranged from "46% of US enterprise token usage" to "three times the American token volume," which are not obviously the same metric — treat both figures as unverified/imprecise). None of this competitive framing is attributed to an on-record OpenAI statement.

5. **Luna's old and new cached-input prices are not confirmed by a primary OpenAI source with an explicit dollar figure.** They are calculated/corroborated only via the general "90% discount on cached reads" rule (stated on the Sol docs page) and a third-party aggregator (aipricing.guru). Treat Luna cached-input figures ($0.10 old, $0.02 new) as lower-confidence than the headline input/output numbers.

6. **Axios headline typo.** The Axios headline reads "OpenAI discounts GPT-5.6 Luna and Terra, but not Luna" — this is almost certainly a publisher error; the article body confirms Luna DID get an 80% cut and that Sol (not Luna) is the one that got no discount. Noted so it isn't mistaken for a substantive discrepancy.

7. **Sol Fast-mode exact price ($10/$60 per 1M tokens) is arithmetic, not an OpenAI-published figure.** Only "2x the price" of standard Sol was found stated directly (via the Altman quote and InfoWorld); the $10/$60 absolute number appears in only one fetched source (Cryptopolitan) and should be treated as a derived estimate rather than a confirmed OpenAI-published rate card entry.

8. **No exact time-of-day for the effective date was found** in any fetched source — only the date July 30, 2026.
