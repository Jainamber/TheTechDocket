## 1. EXTRATERRITORIAL SCOPE — EU AI Act Article 2

**Core rule (from Article 2, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), as quoted by both a reference site and two top law firms:**

- Applies to **providers** "placing AI systems or general-purpose AI models on the market in the EU or putting into service AI systems ... in the EU, **irrespective of whether those providers are established or located within the Union or in a third country**." — [artificialintelligenceact.eu, Article 2 explainer](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/2/)
- Applies to **providers and deployers located in a third country "where the output produced by the AI system is used in the Union"** — same source. Deployer coverage requires professional/business use (not personal, non-professional use). — [Cooley law firm](https://cdp.cooley.com/eu-ai-act-does-it-affect-your-organization-or-not/)
- Cooley's worked example: a US software developer offering a novel AI system in the EU market is in scope as a "provider" purely because it's "making an AI system available on the EU market for the first time" — location is irrelevant.
- **Holland & Knight (top US law firm)** frames it as three concrete triggers for non-EU companies: (1) market placement regardless of where the provider is based; (2) output used/affecting people in the EU even if servers are outside the EU; (3) importing/distributing AI systems into EU-bound products. Explicitly: **"no safe harbor"** — a company doesn't need an EU office or EU employees to be in scope. — [Holland & Knight, Apr 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/04/us-companies-face-eu-ai-acts-possible-august-2026-compliance-deadline)
- Fines for Article 50 (transparency) breaches: **up to €15 million or 3% of global annual turnover**, whichever is higher — [European Commission press release](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_1714); confirmed by [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/). Higher tier for prohibited-practice breaches: **up to €35 million or 7% of turnover** — [aigrants.in](https://aigrants.in/topics/eu-ai-act-compliance-for-indian-startups), consistent with figures cited across law-firm sources.

**Critical nuance for the story — NOT everything starts 2 Aug 2026:**
Via the "Digital Omnibus" agreement between the Council and Parliament (~7 May 2026), high-risk AI obligations were **postponed**:
- Stand-alone high-risk systems (Annex III — e.g. HR/hiring, credit scoring, law enforcement, education) → pushed from 2 Aug 2026 to **2 December 2027**
- AI embedded in already-regulated products (Annex I — e.g. medical devices, machinery, vehicles) → pushed from 2 Aug 2027 to **2 August 2028**
- Member-state AI regulatory sandboxes deadline → pushed to 2 August 2027
(All per [Gibson Dunn, "EU AI Act Omnibus Agreement"](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/))

**What DOES start 2 Aug 2026, on schedule:** Article 50 transparency obligations (deepfake labelling, AI-generated public-interest text disclosure, chatbot/AI-interaction disclosure) and the **EU AI Office becomes fully operational** with investigation and fining powers. Gibson Dunn: "2 August 2026 remains a live compliance date."

## 4. INDIA'S OWN AI REGULATION STATUS (as of 3 Aug 2026)

- **India AI Governance Guidelines** — released by **MeitY** under the **IndiaAI Mission** on **4-5 November 2025** (PIB's own release states "5 November 2025" in one rendering; Digital Policy Alert independently logs "November 4, 2025" — treat as 4–5 Nov 2025, NOT January 2026 as originally hypothesized in the brief). Sources: [PIB press release PRID=2186639](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2186639); [NEGD-hosted PIB PDF, dated 19 Dec 2025, restating the 5 Nov 2025 release](https://negd.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Press-Release_Press-Information-Bureau3-6.pdf); [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/35105-ministry-of-electronics-and-information-technology-released-india-ai-governance-guidelines).
- **Status: voluntary / principles-based, NOT binding law.** Explicitly "principle-based and not prescriptive"; relies on existing statutes (IT Act 2000, DPDP Act 2023, sectoral regulation) rather than a new horizontal AI statute — the government's stated position is that **"a new horizontal AI law is unnecessary at this stage."** (NEGD/PIB PDF)
- **Structure:** "Do No Harm" as the foundational concept; **Seven Sutras** (guiding principles): Trust is the Foundation, People First, Innovation over Restraint, Fairness and Equity, Accountability, Understandable by Design, Safety/Resilience/Sustainability; **six governance pillars**: Infrastructure, Capacity Building, Policy & Regulation, Risk Mitigation, Accountability, Institutions. — [PIB PRID=2228315, 15 Feb 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2228315&reg=48&lang=2) (this later release restates/elaborates the framework, timed around India's AI Impact Summit)
- New institutions stemming from the guidelines: **AI Governance Group (AIGG)**, **Technology & Policy Expert Committee (TPEC)**, **IndiaAI Safety Institute** (same PIB source).
- **13 April 2026**: Government constituted the **AI Governance and Economic Group (AIGEG)** — merging the earlier proposed "AI Governance Group" and "AI Economic Council" into one body. **Chair: Ashwini Vaishnaw** (Union IT Minister); **Vice Chair: Jitin Prasada** (MoS Electronics & IT); members include MeitY Secretary, Principal Scientific Advisor, Chief Economic Advisor, NITI Aayog CEO, telecom/science secretaries, National Security Council Secretariat rep. **MediaNama's reporting flags a governance gap: AIGEG excludes sectoral regulators** — TRAI, CCI, RBI, SEBI, ICMR, UGC, Data Protection Board — despite the Nov 2025 guidelines having recommended broad regulator representation. — [MediaNama, 13 Apr 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/04/223-aigeg-meitys-ai-governance-body-excludes-regulators-recommended-ai-guidelines/)
- **DPDP Rules, 2025**: notified by MeitY on **14 November 2025**, operationalising the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 — India's closest binding, cross-sector tech-compliance regime (breach notification, data-fiduciary audits, 90-day rights-response window, digital Data Protection Board) — with an **18-month phased compliance runway** (rolling into force through roughly mid-2027). Runs in parallel to, not integrated with, the voluntary AI guidelines. — [PIB PDF](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)
- India hosted the **"AI Impact Summit 2026"** in New Delhi (~19–20 Feb 2026 per [newsonair.gov.in](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/ai-india-impact-summit-2026-is-curated-around-three-pillars-people-planet-progress-additional-secretary-meity-abhishek-singh/)); the 15 Feb 2026 PIB restatement of the AI Governance Guidelines appears timed to this summit — not independently confirmed causally, but the date proximity is suggestive.
- **Framing line for the piece:** as of 3 Aug 2026, India's AI regulatory posture remains explicitly **voluntary and principle-based**, contrasting directly with the EU's binding, fine-backed statute that just began active enforcement — this exact contrast is also the one Business Standard draws (see item 6).
