## 4. Table stakes vs. our opportunity

### Facts in EVERY (or nearly every) top disclosure article — table stakes, don't waste words re-explaining without a new angle
- The $94–94.1B headline figure and that it came from the Q2 2026 10-Q
- The lock-up split: ~$80B short-term-restricted / $14.1B locked through Q3 2027
- The 2015 origin story: Google + Fidelity, ~$900M–$1B, SpaceX valued ~$12B at the time
- SpaceX's IPO: June 12, 2026, $135/share, ~$1.77T valuation
- The link to Alphabet's blowout Q2 net income ($112.1B, +298% YoY) via a ~$99B gain on equity securities (SpaceX + Anthropic combined)
- Some version of "Alphabet can't actually sell/spend this yet" (lock-up framing)

### Facts/angles NO top article has — our opportunity
1. **Reconciling the $94.1B (10-Q, post-IPO market price) vs. the earlier $122B (Alaska filing, pre-IPO speculative $2T valuation) headline figures.** Both are real, both are sourced, both circulate under near-identical framing ("Google's SpaceX stake is worth $X billion"), and nobody in the top results explains they're answering different questions at different valuations/dates.
2. **The percentage-figure chaos, sourced and explained.** Across the 5 articles fetched: 7.5% (Yahoo, 2015-era), "just under 10%" (Motley Fool, 2015-era), "approximately 10%" (Analytics Insight, 2015-era), ~6% "before recent dilution" (Motley Fool), ~4.9%–5.0% (Yahoo, Motley Fool), 4–6% (Analytics Insight, which explicitly notes **the 10-Q itself omitted an exact percentage**), 6.11% (Alaska filing, end of 2025), "roughly 6%" (Fortune), "approximately 7%" (Motley Fool ownership piece). No single article surfaces this spread or explains that every percentage in circulation is a media estimate, not a disclosed filing fact — and that dilution from the Feb 2026 SpaceX–xAI merger is a real driver of the drop from ~6% to ~4.9%.
3. **SpaceX's dual-class voting structure.** Musk's 42% economic stake carries 82.4% of the vote (Class B shares). None of the four disclosure-specific articles mention this, even though it directly frames what a "6% stake" means for Google's actual influence (essentially zero governance rights, pure economic exposure) — a natural and currently-missing paragraph for a "what the filing reveals" piece.
4. **Google's position relative to other outside SpaceX shareholders.** Founders Fund ($20M in 2008 → ~$50B), Sequoia, Andreessen Horowitz, Valor Equity Partners (~2% or less each) — establishing that Google's $94B is by far the largest non-founder stake is only in the separate ownership piece, never in the disclosure coverage itself.
5. **A current-price reframe.** Every disclosure article marks the stake at its June 30, 2026 10-Q value ($94.1B); none recompute what it's worth at the post-selloff price (~$114, down 49% from the post-IPO peak, per the July 27 24/7 Wall St piece). A simple "here's what $94.1B is worth today" follow-through is open ground — with the caveat that we could not find a source giving an exact current share count/market cap to compute this cleanly (flagged in Section 3); if we publish a recomputed number we should source our own share-count figure rather than rely on investing.com's stale page.
6. **Naming the accounting mechanism.** Fortune flags that a "paper," non-sellable gain drove $6.26 of Alphabet's $9.11 EPS, and raises a circularity concern for Anthropic specifically — but no article explains the actual GAAP treatment (equity securities without control get marked to market through the income statement) or extends the circularity critique to SpaceX.
7. **The SpaceX↔Google compute-deal connection.** Search results surface a separate, contemporaneous story — Google agreeing to pay SpaceX/xAI ~$920M/month for data-center compute capacity (CNBC and TechCrunch, both June 5, 2026: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/05/google-to-pay-spacex-920-million-a-month-for-xai-compute-capacity.html, https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/05/google-will-pay-spacex-920m-per-month-for-compute/) — that no disclosure article connects to the equity-stake story, even though Fortune explicitly makes this exact "invests in X, X pays Google, X's valuation climbs, Google books it as profit" argument for Anthropic. The parallel structure (Google is simultaneously a SpaceX shareholder *and* a large customer of SpaceX's xAI compute) is sitting in our own search results, unused by any top-ranking article.
