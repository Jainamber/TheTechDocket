## 1. Starlink in India — license status, launch status, pricing, partnerships, caps (as of Jul 2026)

**Regulatory timeline (chronological):**

  retrospective). https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/cleared-for-orbit-stuck-on-the-tarmac-starlinks-long-india-wait/
- May 2025: DoT approves Starlink to begin compliance work / issues Letter of Intent.
  https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/08/starlinks-launch-in-india-now-a-matter-of-when-not-if
- Jun 2025: Starlink receives **GMPCS licence** from DoT — third company to get one after OneWeb and
  Reliance Jio. Conditions included real-time terminal tracking, local data processing, and sourcing 20%
  of ground infrastructure locally (this local-sourcing rule was later dropped, see below).
  https://www.smartprix.com/bytes/starlink-receives-gmpcs-license-from-department-of-telecommunications-dot-could-launch-commercially-by-early-2026/
  https://www.newsonair.gov.in/starlink-receives-license-to-launch-satellite-internet-service-in-india
- Mar 12, 2025: Jio signs distribution/retail pact with SpaceX for Starlink (equipment through Jio retail
  outlets + installation support); Airtel signed a similar deal a day earlier.
  https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/reliance-jio-starlink-spacex-deal-internet-india-bharti-airtel-125031200115_1.html
  https://feeds.bbci.co.uk/news/articles/cqx0qpy3204o
- Jul 8, 2025: **IN-SPACe** grants final authorisation for Starlink's Gen1 constellation (4,408 satellites,
  ~600 Gbps throughput over India), valid 5 years or constellation life, whichever is shorter. Called
  India's "final regulatory nod" at the time (later proved not actually final — see security hurdle below).
  https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/business%2Fmusks-starlink-receives-indias-final-regulatory-nod-for-launch-3622414
- Jul 28-29, 2025: Union MoS for Communications **Pemmasani Chandra Sekhar** states Starlink will be
  capped at **2 million (20 lakh) users** and **200 Mbps** max speed, with monthly cost "around ₹3,000."
  Quote: *"Starlink can have only 20 lakh customers in India and offer up to 200 Mbps speed. That won't
  affect telecom services."* Also: *"The upfront cost for satcom services will be too high, and the
  monthly cost may be around ₹3,000."* — this is a ministerial estimate/framing (made in context of
  reassuring BSNL), **not a confirmed final regulatory instrument** — flagged as such below.
  https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/starlink-can-serve-2-million-users-in-india-at-rs-3000-per-month-125072801284_1.html
  https://ackodrive.com/news/starlink-india-launch-limited-to-20-lakh-users-with-200-mbps-speed-cap-govt/
- Sep 25, 2025: Starlink secures **provisional/trial spectrum** from DoT for testing; reported infra plan
  of 17 ground-station locations, 20 terrestrial gateways; reported pricing at this stage: hardware
  ₹30,000-35,000, monthly ₹3,000-4,200, speeds 25-220 Mbps.
  https://www.lightreading.com/satellite/starlink-eyes-satellite-broadband-rollout-by-year-end-in-india-reports
- Dec 8, 2025: Starlink's own website briefly displayed **₹8,600/month + ₹34,000 one-time hardware**,
  unlimited data, "99.9% uptime," 30-day trial — then removed the pricing, SpaceX calling it a
  "technical glitch" per Economic Times reporting (via Business Standard). Business pricing not
  disclosed. **Disagreement flagged**: this contradicts the ₹2,500-3,500/month figure reported
  separately as the plan Starlink was preparing to launch at.
  https://www.news9live.com/technology/tech-news/starlink-india-price-8600-month-satellite-internet-2910168
  https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/starlink-india-launch-monthly-plans-retail-pricing-satellite-internet-125121000198_1.html
- Feb 16, 2026: Status recap — Starlink still has no official India price; "large-scale commercial
  services anticipated by mid-2026" (did not happen — see below); pending items: security trials, ground
  station requirements, direct-to-device approvals.
  https://yourstory.com/2026/02/spacex-starlink-vs-reliance-jio
- Mar 3, 2026: SpaceX VP Michael Nicolls says direct-to-cell (2nd-gen constellation, ~1,200 sats) will
  begin launching **"mid-2027 with Starship."** No confirmed Indian telecom partner named specifically
  for direct-to-cell. Quote: *"We'll begin launching in mid-2027 with Starship, we'll be able to deploy
  the constellation very quickly."* https://telecomtalk.info/starlink-will-launch-direct-to-cell-satellites/1004812/
- May 2026 (Communications Today retrospective, "Cleared for orbit, stuck on the tarmac"): after 3.5
  years, Starlink has cleared only 2 of 4 regulatory layers. Still pending: (a) **Security clearance**
  — separate clearance per satellite gateway, localised lawful-interception facilities; Takshashila
  Institution flagged switch-off compliance/data-protection concerns; (b) **FDI approval** from
  Department of Space — SpaceX's 100% ownership structure exceeds the 74% automatic-approval threshold
  and needs explicit government clearance; (c) **Spectrum & pricing rules** under the Telecommunications
  Act 2023 remained un-notified — TRAI recommended 4% of AGR, DoT reportedly favoured 5% (with a 1%
  discount for remote-area service). Telecom Minister **Jyotiraditya Scindia** (Feb 2026) said govt was
  "working to ensure that the rollout of satellite services would not suffer from a lack of regulatory
  clarity on spectrum assignment and pricing." Global (non-India) terminal price benchmark cited:
  $250-380 + monthly fees. https://www.broadcastandcablesat.co.in/cleared-for-orbit-stuck-on-the-tarmac-starlinks-long-india-wait/
- Jun 9-10, 2026: **Bloomberg** (paywalled, cross-confirmed via Deccan Herald reprint) reports India's
  Ministry of Home Affairs withheld Starlink's final security clearances, linked to concerns over
  satellite-terminal use during the Iran/West Asia conflict — this hit just before SpaceX's IPO.
  https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-09/starlink-india-launch-hits-security-roadblock-before-spacex-ipo
  https://www.deccanherald.com/business/starlink-india-launch-hits-security-roadblock-before-spacex-ipo-4033748
  https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/india-puts-starlink-launch-on-hold-amid-security-concerns-linked-to-west-asia-conflict-report-535909-2026-06-09
- Jun 18, 2026: DoT publishes **draft Telecommunications (Spectrum Assignment by Administrative
  Process) Rules 2026** (19 user categories, no-auction/administrative assignment, Telecom Act 2023).
  Covers GSO services (DTH, VSAT, teleports, BSNL satphone) but **omits NGSO consumer broadband
  entirely** — Starlink, Eutelsat OneWeb and Jio Satellite Communications get no mention in the fee
  schedule, meaning none of TRAI's May 2025 recommended NGSO charges (4% of AGR) were implemented.
  Comment window ran to Jul 18, 2026. Covered fees: commercial VSAT 3-4% of AGR (speed-dependent),
  BSNL satphone 1% of AGR, application fee ₹1,000.
  https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-dot-draft-spectrum-rules-leave-starlink-rival-broadband-operators-out/
- Jun 22, 2026: **The Week** reports the draft rules add a **second, distinct security-clearance gate**
  even after spectrum assignment/Letter of Intent — operators can hold spectrum yet still be blocked
  from serving customers pending vetting; networks barred from connecting to public telecom systems
  without separate permission. https://www.theweek.in/news/biz-tech/2026/06/22/india-satellite-communication-regulations.amp.html
- Jun 26, 2026: **Inc42** reports the proposed 20%-local-sourcing mandate for ground-segment equipment
  was **dropped**; gateway/lawful-interception/monitoring requirements retained; no revised commercial
  timeline given. Quote (Utkarsh Sinha, Bexley Advisors): *"The removal of the requirement removes a
  major commercial hurdle and shows that India wants to attract global satellite operators."* Quote
  (Mahesh Uppal, ComFirst) on why localisation was dropped: *"Competitive Indian hardware is perhaps
  still not readily available."* https://inc42.com/buzz/india-drops-20-local-sourcing-rule-keeps-security-guardrails-for-satcom/

**Bottom line as of Jul 28, 2026:** No evidence found of an actual Starlink commercial consumer launch
in India. Status is GMPCS licence + IN-SPACe authorisation held, but spectrum pricing/allocation and a
second security-clearance gate remain open as of the most recent (late Jun 2026) reporting found. Any
claim that Starlink has commercially launched in India by this date is **UNVERIFIED / likely false**
based on available sources — treat "2 million user cap, ₹3,000/month" as a Jul 2025 ministerial estimate
for a not-yet-launched service, not a confirmed live tariff.

## 3. India's satellite-internet competitive field, 2026

- **Eutelsat OneWeb (Bharti/Airtel-backed):** Bharti holds >20% of OneWeb (parent Eutelsat); Sunil
  Bharti Mittal is Eutelsat board vice-chairman. OneWeb got IN-SPACe market access **Nov 21, 2023**
  (first operator to do so) with in-principle approval for gateways in Gujarat and Tamil Nadu.
  https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/21/starlink-oneweb-satellite-broadband-india
  Distribution tie-up with **Hughes Communications India** signed Jan 20, 2022 (6-year deal), originally
  targeting mid-2022 launch (missed). https://www.techradar.com/news/bharti-backed-oneweb-collaborates-with-hughes-to-offer-satellite-internet-in-india
  As of the May 2026 retrospective, OneWeb is grouped with Starlink and Jio Satellite as one of the
  **"three operators, one bottleneck"** still stuck behind the same spectrum-pricing/security gap.
  https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/cleared-for-orbit-stuck-on-the-tarmac-starlinks-long-india-wait/
- **Jio-SES joint venture ("Jio Space Technology Limited," commercially branded "Orbit Connect"):**
  51% Jio Platforms / 49% SES, announced Feb 14, 2022. Combines SES-12 (GEO) and O3b mPOWER (MEO)
  capacity, up to 100 Gbps, multi-year capacity deal worth ~$100 million. IN-SPACe approval received
  Jun 2024 — second operator (after OneWeb) to get it; pursuing Ka/Ku-band trial spectrum.
  https://www.ses.com/press-release/jio-platforms-ltd-and-ses-announce-joint-venture-deliver-high-performance-satellite
  https://telecomtalk.info/jio-owned-orbit-connect-gets-in-space-approval-for-offering-satcom-services-report/977836/
  - Quote (Akash Ambani, Jio): *"This new joint venture with SES will further accelerate the growth of
    multigigabit broadband."* Quote (Steve Collar, SES CEO): *"This joint venture with JPL is a great
    example of how SES can complement even the most extensive terrestrial networks."*
- **Jio Satellite Communications Limited (JSCL)** — a separate, wholly/majority Jio-owned legal entity
  (distinct from the SES JV) — received **IN-SPACe approval on Jul 17, 2026** for its own sovereign
  **1,600-satellite LEO constellation**, targeting **4.5-5 Tbps** capacity via 20-22 ground stations.
  IN-SPACe found the proposal "technically sound" after joint evaluation with ISRO and DoT. This
  capacity target (~5 Tbps) is explicitly reported as exceeding **Starlink's approved ~600 Gbps** over
  India. Enables Jio to proceed with international orbital-slot filings. Reported investment scale:
  $10-15 billion for ~1,650 satellites (Fierce Network).
  https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/reliance-jio-gets-in-space-approval-for-1-600-satellite-leo-network-126071700270_1.html
  https://www.fierce-network.com/wireless/can-jio-build-indias-answer-starlink
  - Quote (Akash Ambani, AGM 2026): *"This dual approach will enable Jio to meet India's connectivity
    needs faster, while laying the foundation for the Indian satellite broadband platform of global
    scale."* Quote (Siddhant Cally, Counterpoint Research): *"By integrating satellite capabilities
    with its existing 5G, fiber and digital infrastructure, Jio is positioning itself as a provider of
    end-to-end connectivity across terrestrial and non-terrestrial networks."*
- **Amazon Project Kuiper / "Amazon Leo":** As of Oct 2024, seeking GMPCS licence from DoT and IN-SPACe
  clearance; submitted gateway/network plans under inter-ministerial-committee review; no India launch
  date given. https://www.businessworld.in/article/amazon%E2%80%99s-leo-satellite-play-project-kuiper-eyes-india-awaits-dot-approval-535579
  As of **Jul 17, 2026** (Business Standard, in the JSCL approval story), Kuiper proposes **3 Tbps**
  capacity for India but **"has not yet secured IN-SPACe authorisation."** This is the most current
  Kuiper-India data point found — no confirmed India launch timeline exists as of this research.
- **Regulatory bottleneck common to all four:** all NGSO consumer operators (Starlink, OneWeb, Jio
  Satellite/JSCL — Kuiper isn't even IN-SPACe-cleared yet) are excluded from the Jun 18, 2026 draft
  administrative spectrum-fee rules (see Section 1), meaning none has a priced/allocated spectrum path
  as of the most recent reporting. https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-dot-draft-spectrum-rules-leave-starlink-rival-broadband-operators-out/

## 4. SpaceX-ISRO / SpaceX-India launch business

- **GSAT-20 (also called GSAT-N2):** Launched **Nov 18-19, 2024** on a Falcon 9 from Cape Canaveral —
  the **first time ISRO used SpaceX** for a launch, arranged via ISRO's commercial arm **NSIL** (New
  Space India Limited). Satellite mass **4,700 kg**, exceeding the payload capacity of India's own
  LVM3 ("Bahubali") rocket; France's Arianespace (India's usual heavy-lift partner) had no operational
  rocket available at the time. 14-year design life; provides broadband to remote areas and in-flight
  connectivity. https://www.newsonair.gov.in/isro-successfully-launches-satellite-gsat-20-on-board-spacexs-falcon-9-rocket-in-us
  - Quote (ISRO Chairman S. Somanath): *"it was a very successful launch, the satellite is healthy and
    solar panels have been deployed."*
- **Since GSAT-20 — private Indian companies (not ISRO government satellites) have also used SpaceX:**
  On **Aug 27, 2025**, **Pixxel** (Bengaluru) launched 3 more Firefly hyperspectral imaging satellites
  on a Falcon 9 (bringing its constellation to 6), and **Dhruva Space** (Hyderabad) launched its LEAP-1
  satellite (first commercial mission) on the same rocket.
  https://www.wionews.com/india-news/why-are-indian-firms-picking-paying-spacex-over-isro-to-launch-satellites-1756294391252
  - Reasons cited: SpaceX's per-kg launch cost is "less than half" of India's; SpaceX flew 100+ Falcon
    9 missions in 2025 vs ISRO's typical 3-5 launches/year — firms prefer "the next available rocket,
    than to wait for an ISRO rocket." Quote (PM Narendra Modi, on India's launch-cadence gap): *"barely
    five or six rocket launches from India in a year,"* with an ambition for the private sector to
    reach *"fifty rockets in a year by the year 2030."*
- **No 2026 ISRO (government) satellite launch via SpaceX found.** ISRO's own 2026 launch activity
  (e.g., a PSLV Earth-observation mission that suffered a **second consecutive failure in Jan 2026**,
  losing 16 satellites) appears to be on Indian vehicles, not SpaceX.
  https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/01/12/indias-pslv-suffers-second-consecutive-launch-failure-16-satellites-lost/
  A specific second SpaceX-ISRO (government) launch contract for 2026 is **UNVERIFIED / not found** in
  this research — only the private-company (Pixxel/Dhruva) launches and the original GSAT-20 mission
  are confirmed.

## 5. Can Indian investors buy SpaceX stock post-IPO?

- SpaceX listed on **Nasdaq** (also referenced as "Nasdaq Texas") under ticker **SPCX** on **Jun 12,
  2026**, IPO priced at **$135/share**; closed up ~19% at **~$161** on day one; ~30% of the offering was
  allocated to retail investors via brokerages like Fidelity and Charles Schwab (US retail, not Indian).
  https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/12/spacex-ipo-spcx-live-updates.html
  https://www.nbcnews.com/business/markets/spacex-ipo-stock-price-rcna349760
- **Indian investors could NOT participate in the IPO/allotment directly** — *"India was not listed as
  a public-offer jurisdiction in the IPO documents,"* so standard Indian routes (ASBA, UPI, NSE/BSE,
  regular demat accounts) had no access. https://www.sahi.com/blogs/how-indians-can-invest-in-spacex-shares
- **Post-listing route described:** Indian residents can buy SPCX (now that it trades on Nasdaq) via
  international-investing platforms that offer US-listed stocks, typically routed through the RBI's
  **Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS)**, which caps permitted overseas remittance/investment at
  **$250,000 per person per financial year**. Purchases happen at prevailing market price, not the
  $135 IPO price. https://www.sahi.com/blogs/how-indians-can-invest-in-spacex-shares
- Costs/considerations the source flags (no advice, just what's cited): currency-conversion fees,
  brokerage charges, remittance fees, tax collected at source (TCS) on remittances, capital-gains tax
  on any sale, and foreign-asset disclosure requirements in Indian tax filings.
- No specific broking platform names were given in the fetched source — **UNVERIFIED** which named
  platforms (e.g., international-investing apps) Indian users actually use for this; not stated in the
  source consulted.
