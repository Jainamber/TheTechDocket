# Case Facts: United States v. Samuel Tunick (GrapheneOS Duress-Password Border Wipe Case)

Compiled 2026-07-29. Every fact is followed by its source URL in parentheses. Where outlets
disagree or a fact could not be independently confirmed, it is marked **UNVERIFIED** with the
conflicting claims listed. Docket: *United States v. Tunick*, No. 1:25-cr-00499 (N.D. Ga.).

## 2. Charges, statute, penalty, and step-by-step allegations

- **Statute:** 18 U.S.C. § 2232(a) — "Destruction or removal of property to prevent seizure."
  Confirmed by name and subsection across many outlets.
  (https://www.techspot.com/news/113236-us-prosecutors-charge-atlanta-man-after-grapheneos-phone.html)
  (https://itsfoss.com/news/grapheneos-duress-password-indictment/)
  (https://www.cp24.com/news/world/2026/07/28/american-activist-charged-after-phone-wiped-during-airport-search-in-entirely-unprecedented-case/)
- **Statute text/maximum penalty (verified against the statute itself):** "fined under this title or
  imprisoned not more than five years, or both." Confirmed against Justia's text of 18 U.S.C.
  §2232 (https://law.justia.com/codes/us/1994/title18/parti/chap109/sec2232/), and repeated by
  reporting: "Up to five years imprisonment"
  (https://www.techadvisor.com — not fetched directly, but corroborated independently by:)
  (https://www.androidauthority.com/grapheneos-duress-pin-us-prosecution-3691271/ — "up to five years")
  (https://www.cp24.com/news/world/2026/07/28/american-activist-charged-after-phone-wiped-during-airport-search-in-entirely-unprecedented-case/ — "up to 5 years imprisonment")
  (https://itsfoss.com/news/grapheneos-duress-password-indictment/ — "up to 5 years in prison and/or fine")
  (https://www.404media.co/the-government-hopes-to-set-a-precedent-an-interview-with-the-man-charged-for-allegedly-wiping-his-grapheneos-phone/ — "five years in federal prison")
- **Indictment language (quoted near-verbatim by two independent outlets, strong corroboration):**
  Tunick is alleged to have "knowingly destroy[ed], damage[d], waste[d], dispose[d] of, and
  otherwise [taken] any action to delete the digital contents of a Google Pixel cellular phone,"
  for "the purpose of preventing and impairing the Government's lawful authority" to seize it.
  (https://www.visaverge.com/news/american-citizen-faces-charges-after-erasing-mobile-device-data-at-us-border/)
  (https://cybernews.com/privacy/atlanta-man-border-search-prosecuted-grapheneos/)
- **Primary source document:** The actual indictment is filed on DocumentCloud
  (https://www.documentcloud.org/documents/28513012-samuel-tunick-indictment/) and the defense's
  motion to suppress is also on DocumentCloud
  (https://www.documentcloud.org/documents/28513064-tunicks-motion-to-suppress/), both linked from
  TechCrunch's article. **I could not fetch either document directly — both returned 403/blocked
  errors on repeated attempts.** All indictment/motion language above is therefore sourced to news
  outlets' quotations of those documents, not my own direct read. **Flag as UNVERIFIED-by-direct-read**, though cross-outlet quote matching gives reasonable confidence in the excerpts reproduced above.
- **Step-by-step alleged sequence of events** (synthesized from TechCrunch, the motion to suppress
  as quoted by Techdirt, and 404 Media's interview with Tunick):
  1. Tunick landed at Hartsfield-Jackson after a trip to the Dominican Republic and was pulled into
     CBP secondary inspection. (https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/24/us-accuses-american-of-allegedly-wiping-his-phone-using-a-duress-password-during-border-search/)
  2. He declined a bag/body search; agents said they were "looking for anything that's
     prohibited," per CBP officer Larry Findley. (https://www.cp24.com/news/world/2026/07/28/american-activist-charged-after-phone-wiped-during-airport-search-in-entirely-unprecedented-case/)
     (https://wegotthiscovered.com/true-crime/atlanta-man-who-wiped-his-phone-during-border-search-may-face-years-in-prison-cbp-officer-says-they-were-looking-for-anything-thats-prohibited/)
  3. Agents told him "because you're refusing to talk, which is your right, we have the authority
     to go through your phone" and "when it comes to immigration and customs...we have search
     authority, we don't need a warrant," and that it was "a whole different ballgame" at the
     border — quotes attributed to the motion to suppress. (https://www.techdirt.com/2026/07/27/protester-charged-with-destroying-property-after-giving-cbp-agents-his-grapheneos-duress-mode-passcode/)
     (https://www.itsfoss.com/news/grapheneos-duress-password-indictment/)
  4. Per the defense, Tunick requested an attorney multiple times (reported as "four times" by the
     Change.org petition) and was refused each time; he was not read Miranda rights.
     (https://www.change.org/p/end-the-federal-case-against-sam-tunick-before-it-sets-a-dangerous-precedent)
     (https://www.techdirt.com/2026/07/27/protester-charged-with-destroying-property-after-giving-cbp-agents-his-grapheneos-duress-mode-passcode/)
  5. Agents demanded phone access, stating (per Tunick's own account) that they were looking for
     child sexual abuse material (CSAM). (https://www.404media.co/the-government-hopes-to-set-a-precedent-an-interview-with-the-man-charged-for-allegedly-wiping-his-grapheneos-phone/)
  6. Tunick provided a passcode. Upon entry, "the screen went blank, flashed several times and the
     phone appeared to restart" — this exact description is used by TechCrunch and repeated
     nearly verbatim by many downstream outlets, suggesting it originates in the motion to
     suppress/complaint. (https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/24/us-accuses-american-of-allegedly-wiping-his-phone-using-a-duress-password-during-border-search/)
  7. The phone's data was wiped (GrapheneOS's "duress password" feature, which performs an
     irreversible wipe when a secondary code is entered instead of the real unlock code).
     (https://grapheneos.org/features#duress — cited via TechCrunch's own outbound link)
  8. CBP seized the now-blank phone anyway and allowed Tunick to enter the country.
     (https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/24/us-accuses-american-of-allegedly-wiping-his-phone-using-a-duress-password-during-border-search/)

## 3. Why CBP stopped him / what they said they were investigating

- **Stated/official reason (per CBP, as relayed in reporting):** Agents said they were searching
  for child sexual abuse material (CSAM). No supporting evidence for this suspicion was presented
  according to multiple outlets. (https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/24/us-accuses-american-of-allegedly-wiping-his-phone-using-a-duress-password-during-border-search/)
  (https://www.usnews.com/news/u-s-news-decision-points/articles/2026-07-27/border-battle-when-wiping-your-own-phone-is-a-crime)
- **Defense's alleged real reason:** Tunick's association with "Defend the Atlanta Forest" /
  "Stop Cop City," the movement opposing the Atlanta Public Safety Training Center ("Cop City").
  Defense says a Homeland Security agent circulated an email roughly three hours before Tunick's
  flight landed, identifying him as "under investigation for suspected terrorism activities" and
  routing that flag to CBP's Tactical Terrorism Response Team and the FBI's Atlanta Joint
  Terrorism Task Force. (https://www.techdirt.com/2026/07/27/protester-charged-with-destroying-property-after-giving-cbp-agents-his-grapheneos-duress-mode-passcode/)
  (https://www.usnews.com/news/u-s-news-decision-points/articles/2026-07-27/border-battle-when-wiping-your-own-phone-is-a-crime)
  (https://www.cp24.com/news/world/2026/07/28/american-activist-charged-after-phone-wiped-during-airport-search-in-entirely-unprecedented-case/ — "placed on a watchlist for 'suspected terrorism activities'")
  - The CBP unit involved is separately identified as the "Tactical Terrorism Response Team."
    (https://www.visaverge.com/news/american-citizen-faces-charges-after-erasing-mobile-device-data-at-us-border/)
    (https://www.privacyguides.org/news/2025/12/10/atlanta-activist-charged-with-wiping-phone-before-cbp-search/)
- Defense characterizes the CSAM questioning as a pretext for "a fishing expedition into Mr.
  Tunick's connections" to the Cop City opposition movement.
  (https://www.usnews.com/news/u-s-news-decision-points/articles/2026-07-27/border-battle-when-wiping-your-own-phone-is-a-crime)
- **Note:** This is a contested characterization, not an agreed fact — CBP/DOJ have not
  substantively responded on the record to confirm or deny the "real reason" framing. TechCrunch
  notes DOJ did not respond to its request for comment.
  (https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/24/us-accuses-american-of-allegedly-wiping-his-phone-using-a-duress-password-during-border-search/)
