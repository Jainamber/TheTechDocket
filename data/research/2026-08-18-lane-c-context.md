## 2. Where the Ohio (Pike County/Piketon) deal fits

1. **Lordstown, OH** — SoftBank + OpenAI, announced Sept 2025 as part of the "five new sites" wave, targeted operational ~2027. [HIGH, see Section 1 sources]
2. **Pike County/Piketon, OH (PORTS)** — the subject of the Aug 17, 2026 Nvidia financing news. SB Energy + Nvidia + OpenAI + US Department of Energy. This is NOT part of the original five-site September 2025 announcement in the sources retrieved.

Pike County/PORTS specifics:
- The site is the **PORTS-Pike Technology Campus**, on the grounds of the former **Portsmouth Gaseous Diffusion Plant** — a Cold War-era uranium enrichment facility built in the 1950s that produced enriched/weapons-grade uranium for the Atomic Energy Commission, the US nuclear weapons program, and Navy submarines. The land is **federally owned by the US Department of Energy**. - Scale: earlier DCD reporting (pre-Aug 17) described SB Energy as in talks to lease OpenAI a **10 GW** data center at the site — the full PORTS/Piketon complex has been described elsewhere as a candidate for "the world's largest data center," powered in part by **9.2 GW of new natural gas plants** built on-site. The Aug 17 OpenAI/Nvidia announcement itself specifies OpenAI's own slice at **up to ~8 GW** (4.25 GW initial + 3.75 GW option). ## 3. Power/grid reality

**National data-center power demand (Goldman Sachs, official research, cited widely):** [HIGH]
- US data-center power demand: **31 GW (2025) → 41 GW (2026) → 66 GW (2027)**.
- US data-center capacity to reach **~95 GW by end-2027**, more than double the end-2025 level (assumes ~70% utilization).
- Year-over-year capacity additions: **13.6 GW in 2026**, **36.3 GW in 2027** (vs. 6.4 GW in 2024 and 8.5 GW in 2025 realized).
- Data centers' share of total US peak summer power demand: **4.1% (2025) → 5.3% (2026) → 8.5% (2027)**.
- Goldman flags a **structural power shortfall of 9.3 GW in 2026, widening to 45 GW by 2028**.
- Goldman Sachs (official): https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/us-data-center-power-demand-projected-to-double-by-2027
- American Public Power Association (citing Goldman): https://www.publicpower.org/periodical/article/us-data-center-power-demand-expected-climb-41-gw-2026-goldman-sachs
- **EIA context**: data centers could use **one-third of US commercial power by 2050**. [MEDIUM — this figure came via a World Resources Institute summary of EIA findings rather than a directly retrieved EIA primary document: https://www.wri.org/insights/us-data-centers-electricity-demand]

**Ohio-specific grid reality (AEP Ohio / PJM):** [HIGH — multiple outlets converge on the core numbers]
- AEP Ohio faces **30 GW of data-center interconnection requests** against a **9.4 GW state peak load** — though AEP's own working forecast, based only on data centers that have completed an interconnection engineering study, is currently **~13 GW**. — Utility Dive: https://www.utilitydive.com/news/aep-ohio-data-center-load-tariff-oma-manufacturers/811583/ ; mgrid.org: https://mgrid.org/2025/12/03/aep-ohio-data-center-tariff-sets-national-precedent-after-30-gw-interconnection-surge/
- AEP Ohio has an **85% data-center tariff** in force since **July 23, 2025**, designed to shift infrastructure costs to data-center developers and off residential ratepayers — described as setting a "national precedent." [MEDIUM-HIGH — mgrid.org + Ohio Capital Journal]
- Ohio's state utility regulator (PUCO) has a standing public resource page on data centers and electricity, confirming this is an active regulatory topic: https://puco.ohio.gov/utilities/electricity/resources/data-centers [HIGH — official state source, though content depth not fully extracted in this pass]

**Electricity-price effects reported in 2026:** [MEDIUM — figures are consistent but come from two overlapping-but-distinct PJM capacity-year auctions, not fully cross-verified against each other]
- The PJM 2026/2027 Base Residual Auction cleared at the **FERC price cap of $329.17/MW-day**, a **22% increase** over the prior record ($269.92/MW-day). Reported effect: consumers across PJM's 13-state footprint (including Ohio) could see electric bills rise **1.5%–5% starting summer 2026**. — Yahoo: https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/energy-bills-likely-tick-again-084017412.html
- Separately, for the **June 2025–May 2026 PJM capacity year**, Ohio residential customers were reported facing roughly **10–15% bill increases** (~2 cents/kWh) tied to data-center-driven capacity-auction results. — Ohio Capital Journal: https://ohiocapitaljournal.com/2025/12/11/energy-market-watchers-see-ohio-electric-bills-rising-as-data-center-demand-continues/
- Ohio manufacturers (Ohio Manufacturers' Association) have publicly disputed AEP Ohio's data-center load forecasts as inflated, even after AEP halved an earlier projection — a sign of active, contested rate-case politics around this exact load growth. [MEDIUM — Utility Dive, same article as above]
