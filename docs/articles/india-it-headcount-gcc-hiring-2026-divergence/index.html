India IT Headcount vs. GCC Hiring in 2026: The Real Split

India's top five IT firms shed 6,981 net roles in FY26, yet GCCs leased 19.2M sq ft in H1 2026. Q2 headcount shifts, EPFO ceiling math, and hiring reality.

India’s five largest IT services exporters—TCS, Infosys, Wipro, HCLTech, and Tech Mahindra—logged a combined net reduction of 6,981 employees in FY26, reversing their 12,718 net additions a year earlier, per NDTV Profit. Yet overall Indian tech employment still grew by 1.4 lakh to 59 lakh in 2026 as Global Capability Centres absorbed 45% of India’s Grade-A office leasing.

Inside the Big Four IT Services Divergence

Quarterly corporate filings for the September 2026 quarter show India’s legacy IT services bellwethers are no longer moving in lockstep on hiring or attrition. According to People Matters, HCLTech led sequential additions with 6,450 net hires—backed by 6,136 freshers—while Wipro shed 6,716 roles, a 2.8% quarterly decline.

Those September numbers highlight why equating annual net headcount drops with mass layoffs misreads how Indian IT services manage bench utilization. During FY26, TCS recorded a net headcount decline of 23,460 and Tech Mahindra shed 1,108 roles, pulling the top-five aggregate negative even though Infosys, Wipro, and HCLTech added modestly, per NDTV Profit. By the September quarter, however, TCS expanded sequentially by 4,258 to 598,056 employees while lifting learning hours 17% to 17.1 million, and Infosys expanded its software-professional ranks from 310,039 to 314,508 while holding employee benefit expenses at ₹25,483 crore, according to People Matters.

Still, the structural hiring engine inside traditional outsourcing has cooled compared with prior cycles. Staffing firm Xpheno tracked 110,000 active Indian tech openings in April 2026, down 8% month-on-month, with IT services accounting for 43,000 openings—slipping below a 50% share of active tech hiring for the first time since December 2025, per NDTV Profit. Enterprises are demanding smaller, AI-native delivery pods rather than linear pyramid staffing, mirroring the developer productivity shifts we tracked in Claude Code vs Pi.

Why GCC Office Leases Tell a Stronger Hiring Story

While listed IT exporters manage margins tightly, multinational Global Capability Centres (GCCs) leased 19.2 million sq. ft. in H1 2026—45% of India’s 42.6 million sq. ft. Grade-A office market, up from 41% a year earlier, per Anarock data in Fortune India.

Because private GCCs rarely publish India-specific quarterly payroll counts, Grade-A commercial leasing acts as a verifiable physical proxy for seat expansion. At standard Indian enterprise density of 80 to 100 sq. ft. per workstation, 19.2 million sq. ft. of H1 2026 GCC commitments represents physical capacity for roughly 192,000 to 240,000 desks—backing Nasscom’s estimate that total Indian tech employment rose by 1.4 lakh to 59 lakh in 2026 despite IT services caution, per NDTV Profit and Fortune India.

Geographic concentration is sharpening as well. Bengaluru led India with 10.8 million sq. ft. of gross leasing in H1 2026, of which GCCs accounted for 70% (roughly 7.55 million sq. ft.), pushing city vacancy down to 10.8% from 12.4% while net absorption jumped 26% year-over-year to 8.27 million sq. ft., per Fortune India. Hyderabad posted 6.4 million sq. ft. of gross leasing (48% GCC share, net absorption up 24% YoY to 5.2 million sq. ft.), and Chennai recorded a 55% GCC share across 3.2 million sq. ft., while average national Grade-A rents climbed 9% year-over-year to ₹96 per sq. ft. as vacancy tightened to 15%, according to Fortune India.

That sustained real-estate spend directly reflects what multinationals are building in India: core AI engineering, cloud architecture, quantitative finance, and security operations centers—roles increasingly intersecting with compliance frameworks we covered in India’s DPDP Act Explained and CERT-In CIAD-2026-0062. How to Track India Tech Hiring Without Getting Misled

Reading India’s tech labor market accurately requires cross-checking three independent signals rather than relying on job-portal headlines or unverified layoff rumors. Listed IT quarterly filings capture services demand, registered commercial leases expose GCC seat expansion, and statutory filings catch formal payroll shifts.

Each signal fills a blind spot left by the others. Quarterly disclosures from TCS, Infosys, HCLTech, and Wipro verify net headcount and voluntary attrition every 90 days, but they miss private product companies and captive multinational R&D centers. Conversely, startup layoff trackers highlight sudden venture-backed cuts, yet they omit quieter off-roll staffing reductions and bench exits across services firms. Pairing verified commercial leasing data—such as Bengaluru’s 70% GCC share in H1 2026 reported by Fortune India—with active requisition counts from firms like Xpheno gives engineers and hiring managers a grounded read on where actual budgets are expanding.

The India Angle

India’s biggest 2026 statutory payroll shift arrived on September 17 when the mandatory EPFO wage ceiling rose from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 per month under Union Cabinet approval, bringing over 51 lakh additional workers into mandatory EPF, EPS, and EDLI coverage.

Here is the exact per-employee compliance math confirmed by KPMG’s October 2026 EPFO FAQ alert:

  • Mandatory employer contribution jump: Employers match 12% of statutory PF wages (3.67% to EPF and 8.33% to EPS), lifting the mandatory monthly employer match from ₹1,800 to ₹3,000—an incremental ₹1,200 per month, or ₹14,400 per year (about $155 at ₹92.68/USD) for every employee previously capped at ₹15,000, per KPMG.
  • Scale impact on mid-size staffing firms: For an Indian IT staffing or support vendor employing 1,000 personnel in the ₹15,000–₹25,000 wage band, mandatory employer PF outflow rises by ₹12 lakh per month, or ₹1.44 crore annually—though eligible net new hires can partly offset costs via up to ₹3,000 per month in Pradhan Mantri Viksit Bharat Rozgar Yojana (PMVBRY) incentives, per KPMG.
  • CTC compliance guardrail: EPFO’s FAQs explicitly warn that Cost-to-Company (CTC) is not a statutory concept for PF liability; employers cannot simply deduct their mandatory employer share from an employee’s statutory wages, and September 2026 payrolls had to be split across two periods (September 1–16 at ₹15,000 and September 17–30 at ₹25,000), per KPMG.

What to Watch

Watch Q3 FY27 headcount and campus-onboarding disclosures from TCS, Infosys, HCLTech, and Wipro in January 2027. After HCLTech onboarded 6,136 freshers while Wipro cut 6,716 roles in the September quarter, the next earnings cycle will test whether fresher hiring broadens across all four majors.

Second, track whether GCC leasing momentum holds into H2 2026 as average Grade-A rents climb past ₹96 per sq. ft. and developers deliver tighter new office supply (down 10% YoY to 22.15 million sq. ft. in H1 2026). If Bengaluru vacancy stays near 10.8% while NCR and MMR lag, expect more multinational engineering centers to expand secondary hubs in Hyderabad, Chennai, and Pune—and keep separating fresh campus hires from the 51 lakh+ EPFO wage-ceiling additions whenever monthly Indian payroll headlines hit the news.

Frequently asked questions

Are Indian IT services companies still shrinking overall headcount?

Not uniformly. While the five largest Indian IT services exporters posted a combined net headcount drop in the prior fiscal year, recent quarterly disclosures show sharp divergence, with HCLTech, Infosys, and TCS adding employees sequentially while Wipro reduced its workforce.

Why do analysts track Grade-A office leasing to measure GCC hiring?

Private multinational Global Capability Centres in India rarely publish quarterly India-only headcount filings. Registered long-term commercial office leases provide a hard physical leading indicator of seat expansion in engineering, cybersecurity, and research hubs across Bengaluru, Hyderabad, and Chennai.

Does India new provident fund wage ceiling reduce take-home salary for all tech workers?

No. The revised statutory ceiling primarily affects employees earning inside the newly covered mid-wage band and existing members whose contributions were capped at the older threshold. Senior software engineers already contributing on higher basic pay face no automatic change unless their employer restructures allowances.
