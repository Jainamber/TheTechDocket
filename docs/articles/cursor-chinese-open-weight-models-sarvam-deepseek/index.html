Cursor Weighs Dropping Chinese AI as Sarvam Sells DeepSeek

A 50-page US House report reveals Cursor, Airbnb, and DoorDash rely on Chinese open-weight models, even as India's Sarvam hosts DeepSeek at 450B tokens daily.
By Saurab Jain · Published November 22, 2026 ·6 min read

SpaceX-owned coding platform Cursor has told US lawmakers it will explore phasing out Chinese open-weight models after building Composer 2 on Moonshot AI’s Kimi K2.5, according to a 50-page joint US House committee report detailed by MACCUS and The Next Web. The report reveals that Airbnb handles 80,000 chats daily on Alibaba’s Qwen and DoorDash deployed 25 Chinese model families, while in India, sovereign AI startup Sarvam is moving the opposite way—serving over 450 billion tokens a day on DeepSeek V4.1 Flash from domestic servers.

Inside the 50-Page US House Investigation Into Cursor, Airbnb, and DoorDash

Released on November 20, 2026, by the House Committee on Homeland Security and the House Select Committee on China, the 50-page report titled Beijing’s AI Playbook documents how deeply Chinese open-weight models sit inside top US software companies, as summarized by MACCUS. Following formal inquiry letters sent on April 29 and July 31, 2026, Airbnb, DoorDash, and Cursor developer Anysphere turned over internal engineering records showing that a six-to-30-fold price advantage drove them to Chinese weights.

For Anysphere, which SpaceX acquired in August 2026 at an implied $60 billion equity valuation according to explainx.ai, the scrutiny centered on Composer 2, Cursor’s proprietary coding model. The committees noted that Anysphere trained Composer 2 on top of Moonshot AI’s Kimi K2.5, a lineage that became public only in March 2026 after an external developer identified the base architecture, per AI Chat Daily. Anysphere informed lawmakers it plans to explore phasing out Chinese-developed open-weight models over geopolitical risk, though it has set no timeline or replacement model.

The consumer disclosures in the report are even more concrete. Airbnb runs customized versions of Alibaba’s Qwen models on US servers to power an assistant handling roughly 80,000 guest and host conversations daily and a second pipeline summarizing 250,000 daily support cases and calls, according to MACCUS. DoorDash told the committees it authorized and deployed 25 Chinese model families, each with at least $5,000 in compute spend, and estimated that ripping out one deeply integrated model would take up to six months.

The 6x to 30x Token Economics Driving Open-Weight Adoption

According to the benchmark table compiled in the House report and verified by MACCUS, a standard workload of 1 million input tokens plus 1 million output tokens costs $1.15 on Alibaba’s Qwen3-235B and $2.74 on DeepSeek-R1, compared with $30.00 on Anthropic’s Claude Opus 4.8 and $35.00 on OpenAI’s GPT-5.5. That pricing gap explains why Chinese weights accounted for 41% of all Hugging Face downloads over the trailing 12 months cited in the report, and why six Chinese models ranked ahead of the top US open-weight model (10th place) in the September 2026 Artificial Analysis rankings.

The savings are not theoretical. As noted by MACCUS, digital bank Revolut self-hosts Zhipu’s open-weight GLM model for internal software engineering and reported that its AI coding bill dropped to one-tenth (a 90% reduction) of what it previously paid Anthropic and OpenAI.

Why US Lawmakers Say Local Hosting Is Not Enough

Hosting Chinese open-weight models on domestic US servers stops prompt data from flowing to Beijing, yet the House committees argue that local hosting cannot fix embedded model behavior or supply-chain lock-in, producing 12 policy recommendations detailed by MACCUS. The report cites CrowdStrike testing showing DeepSeek’s reasoning model was up to 50% more likely to generate code with severe security flaws when prompts touched topics politically sensitive to Beijing, alongside an Anthropic and NIST analysis where $4,400 of compute stripped Zhipu GLM-5.3’s safety refusals from 95% down to 3%–6%.

Commercial licensing traps also worry lawmakers. Moonshot AI’s Kimi K3 license requires any commercial SaaS provider exceeding $20 million in trailing 12-month revenue to negotiate a separate paid agreement with Moonshot, according to MACCUS. To break that dependency, the committees recommend extending the October 2026 federal DeepSeek ban across all Chinese models (and fine-tuned or quantized derivatives) in federal contracts and critical infrastructure, mandating a NIST “AI bill of materials,” and funding a national US open-weight initiative.

The India Angle

While Washington debates banning Chinese weights, Indian sovereign AI unicorn Sarvam has launched its “Token Factory” service hosting DeepSeek, Moonshot, and Z.ai models on Indian servers, already processing more than 450 billion tokens per day on DeepSeek V4.1 Flash alone, according to Bloomberg reporting cited by The Next Web. The move sparked immediate debate in India because Sarvam receives subsidized compute backing under the government’s IndiaAI Mission and recently signed a defence ministry AI contract, though co-founders Pratyush Kumar and Vivek Raghavan stated that Token Factory runs on chips funded entirely by private capital.

For Indian engineering teams, the arithmetic explains why Sarvam’s domestic hosting is drawing 450 billion tokens a day. At the November 21, 2026 exchange rate of ₹92.683 per US dollar reported by Wise, an Indian startup processing 500 million input tokens and 500 million output tokens per month on a USD-billed closed frontier API like OpenAI GPT-5.5 ($35.00 per 1M+1M bundle per MACCUS) pays $17,500 before fees, which balloons to ₹19,80,810 per month (₹2.38 crore per year) once a 3.5% bank forex markup and 18% IGST are applied.

Running that exact same 1-billion-token monthly workload on Qwen3-235B ($1.15 per 1M+1M bundle) or DeepSeek-R1 ($2.74 per bundle) cuts the effective cost to ₹65,085 or ₹1,55,070 per month respectively—a 92% to 96.7% cost reduction, using the MACCUS benchmark rates and Wise spot FX. When served domestically in rupees by an Indian provider like Sarvam, teams also eliminate the 3.5% foreign-card fee, claim full 18% GST input tax credit, and keep customer prompts inside Indian jurisdiction under the DPDP Act rules we analyzed in The Real Cost of Running AI and DeepSeek API Price Hike.

What to Watch

Three near-term checkpoints will show whether Washington’s crackdown reshapes global developer tooling or accelerates a split between US federal compliance stacks and the rest of the world:

  1. Cursor’s Composer 3 Base-Model Decision: Watch whether SpaceX-backed Cursor migrates Composer off Moonshot’s Kimi K2.5 onto a Grok base model, a new US open-weight release like Reflection AI’s Beam or Thinking Machines Lab’s Inkling 2 Mini, or keeps Kimi for non-federal commercial tiers, as tracked by explainx.ai.
  2. NIST’s AI Bill of Materials & Federal Contractor Rules: Indian IT services exporters and SaaS startups serving US federal agencies or US critical infrastructure must audit their fine-tuned and quantized models now; if Congress adopts the House report’s derivative-model ban reported by MACCUS, any wrapper built on Qwen, Kimi, or DeepSeek weights will require a six-month swap plan.
  3. IndiaAI Mission Policy on Dual-Track Hosting: Watch whether India’s Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) issues formal provenance guidelines separating publicly subsidized sovereign model training from privately funded domestic inference clouds like Sarvam’s 450-billion-token-per-day DeepSeek service reported by The Next Web.

Frequently asked questions

Why is Cursor considering phasing out Chinese open-weight AI models?

Anysphere, the developer of Cursor now owned by SpaceX, informed two US House committees that it plans to explore phasing out Chinese-developed open-weight models over geopolitical risk after building its Composer 2 model on Moonshot AI Kimi base weights.

How do Airbnb and DoorDash use Chinese open-weight models?

According to the joint US House report, Airbnb runs customized versions of Alibaba Qwen on US servers to handle customer chats and summarize support tickets, while DoorDash evaluated or deployed models across multiple Chinese open-weight families.

Why is India's Sarvam hosting DeepSeek and Moonshot models?

Sarvam launched its Token Factory platform using privately funded chips to serve open-weight models like DeepSeek and Moonshot inside Indian data centers, keeping developer prompts and customer data within India while offering low-cost token inference.
