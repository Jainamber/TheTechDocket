Gemini 4 Neon Benchmarks: Speed, API Cost & Antigravity Bug

Google DeepMind's Gemini 4 Neon scores 61 on Artificial Analysis and 87.4 on LiveBench 2.0 at $3/$15 per 1M tokens, while Antigravity Pro users hit desyncs.

Google DeepMind has released Gemini 4 Neon, its fourth frontier model in seven weeks, scoring 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index and 87.4 on LiveBench 2.0 at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens. While Neon cuts task costs by 17% below Gemini 4 Carbon, Google AI Pro subscribers report Antigravity IDE entitlement lockouts and cache-miss quota drain.

How Gemini 4 Neon Scores Across Frontier Benchmarks

Independent evaluations published through November 22, 2026 show Gemini 4 Neon ranking first on LiveBench 2.0 at 87.4 overall and third on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at 61, trailing only Anthropic’s Claude Fable 5.2 and OpenAI’s GPT-6.2 Astra per Artificial Analysis and AIFOD’s AI SiteFlow review.

Rather than a single vendor sweeping every evaluation, the late November 2026 leaderboard divides across three distinct domains, per AIFOD’s analysis of 17 frontier models. Google’s Gemini 4 Neon leads browser automation at 95.6 and research synthesis at 94.5 on LiveBench 2.0, completing runs in 22.3 minutes at an average cost of $2.654 per task. Anthropic’s Claude Fable 5.2 leads the LMSYS Chatbot Arena at 1,448 Elo and tops the nine-benchmark Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0.1 at 65 in Max Effort mode ($26.54 per task, 42.7 minutes). OpenAI’s GPT-6.2 Astra leads LiveBench 2.0 software engineering with a 91.9 coding score, finishing tasks in 15.1 minutes at $1.941 per run.

Inside Google’s own lineup across our AI Models hub, Gemini 4 Neon eliminates the generation bottleneck that slowed Gemini 4 Carbon. According to Artificial Analysis benchmark testing, Neon streams 104 to 110 output tokens per second versus Carbon’s 57 tokens per second, trimming average Intelligence Index task time by 40% from 36 minutes down to 21 to 22.8 minutes. Even though Neon emits roughly 15% more output tokens per task than Carbon (283 million total output tokens across the suite versus the 65.4 million peer median), its 25% lower standard token pricing ($3/$15 per million tokens versus Carbon’s $4/$20 standard rate) cuts average evaluation cost by 17% from $7.76 to $6.44 per task, per the Artificial Analysis model page. Carbon’s temporary $2/$10 promotional pricing expires on November 30, 2026.

Coding Agents, Visual Reasoning, and Verbosity Trade-Offs

Tested inside Google’s Antigravity CLI, Gemini 4 Neon (xhigh) scores 59 on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index at $6.86 per task, beating Gemini 4 Carbon (55) and matching Claude Fable 5.2 (58) and Claude Opus 5.5 (58) at less than half their execution cost per Artificial Analysis.

For engineering teams automating multi-file refactoring and test generation, per-task completion cost matters more than raw token prices—a dynamic we detailed in the real cost of running AI. Running the same Coding Agent Index in Claude Code costs $12.61 per task on Claude Opus 5.5 (max) and $15.47 per task on Claude Fable 5.2 (xhigh), meaning Gemini 4 Neon ($6.86) lowers agentic coding spend by 45.6% against Opus 5.5 and 55.7% against Fable 5.2, per Artificial Analysis. Neon offers a 1-million-token context window with text, image, audio, and video inputs across four effort settings (low, medium, high, and xhigh), where low costs $0.75 per task in 3.7 minutes and high scores 60 at $3.74 in 14.8 minutes (Artificial Analysis).

Visual reasoning and document presentation show the largest gains over earlier releases like Gemini 3.6 Flash. On GDP.pdf, Gemini 4 Neon (high) scores 33.4%, jumping 5 percentage points above Gemini 4 Carbon (high, 28.4%), while reaching 1,700 Elo overall and 1,771 Elo for Presentation Quality on AA-Briefcase—45 points ahead of prior leader Claude Sonnet 5.5 (max, 1,726 Elo), per Artificial Analysis. On Google’s Multimodal Knowledge Agent (MKA) benchmark, Neon hits a 56% Climb Win-Stay Rate, matching Claude Fable 5.2 (57%). The main trade-off at xhigh effort is a 59.30-second time to first token and high verbosity (3.482% output-to-input ratio across 8.12 billion input tokens), making local models like Inkling 2 Mini faster for small inner-loop edits (Artificial Analysis).

Inside the Antigravity Entitlement Desync and Cache Eviction Bugs

Despite Google advertising Gemini 4 Neon across Antigravity IDE for Pro and Ultra plans, developer threads from November 19 to November 23, 2026 document server-side entitlement lockouts and a prompt-cache eviction bug that burns 58% of weekly quota in three hours, per the Google AI Developers Forum.

Three distinct server-side issues affect developers on Antigravity v1.5.14:

  1. Direct-Paid Pro Entitlement Desync: Paid Google AI Pro subscribers (including 5 TB Google One plans) report that while Gemini 4 Neon, Hydrogen, and Argon work on gemini.google.com, the desktop Antigravity IDE grays out all Gemini 4 models as Unavailable and restricts sessions to Gemini 3.8 Flash, per November 22 forum bug reports. Re-authenticating OAuth and resetting onboarding do not fix the stalled backend userTier sync.
  2. Reliance Jio Partner Bundle Exclusion & Third-Party Sunset: Indian subscribers on the Reliance Jio Google AI Pro bundle found all Gemini 4 models blocked in Antigravity alongside a notice that third-party models—Claude Sonnet 4.6 (Thinking), Claude Opus 4.6 (Thinking), and GPT-OSS 120B (Medium)—will be removed on November 30, 2026, per the Jio bundle thread. On November 20, 2026, Google support confirmed that the Jio bundle only grants Pro’s 5-hour refreshing quota on Gemini 3.8 Flash and excludes Gemini 4 unless users supply a paid GEMINI_API_KEY in Antigravity CLI (v1.5.13+).
  3. Unchanged-Prefix Cache Collapse Above 250K Context: SQLite logs from ~/.gemini/antigravity/conversations/*.db on November 22, 2026—after Google’s November 21 cache patch—reveal severe server-side cache eviction (antigravity-cli Issue #1720), per developer telemetry. Across 385 calls (48.7 million uncached input tokens, 39.6 million cache-read tokens), 141 calls above 50K context suffered zero cache reads, generating 45.1 million uncached tokens. Miss rates jump from 10% at 50K–100K tokens (92 calls) and 29% at 100K–250K (100 calls) to 43% at 250K–300K (51 calls) and 65% at 300K–500K (123 calls), multiplying input token costs 20x from the $0.15 cached rate to the $3.00 uncached rate (Artificial Analysis).

Frequently asked questions

What is Gemini 4 Neon and how does it differ from Gemini 4 Carbon?

Gemini 4 Neon is Google DeepMind fourth frontier release in seven weeks, offering a full multimodal input pipeline across text, image, audio, and video with four configurable reasoning effort tiers. Compared with Gemini 4 Carbon, Neon cuts standard token pricing, doubles generation throughput, and finishes complex agentic evaluations faster.

Why are Gemini 4 models showing as unavailable in Google Antigravity on Jio Pro plans?

Google support confirmed on the developer forum that partner promotional bundles such as the Reliance Jio Google AI Pro offer grant higher quotas on Gemini 3.8 Flash without unlocking the Gemini 4 family inside Antigravity IDE. In addition, direct-paid Google AI Pro subscribers have reported backend account-tier synchronization failures.

Why do multi-turn coding sessions in Antigravity burn through weekly quota so quickly?

Telemetry logs shared by developers on the Google AI Developers Forum show that long-context conversations frequently suffer full server-side prompt cache misses on unchanged prefixes. When a cache read drops to zero on a large repository context, the entire prompt is re-billed as uncached input tokens.
