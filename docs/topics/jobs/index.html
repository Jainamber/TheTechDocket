Definitions & How to Read Scores

Quick reference for confidence tiers, country observability grades, and the 0–100 audit scores used across this tracker.

Category Label / Range What It Means
Confidence Tiers Tier 1 Statutory & Government Filings: Official payrolls and legal notices (US BLS CES/JOLTS, State WARN logs, SEC Form 8-K/6-K, India EPFO/ESIC/PLFS, SEBI Reg 30). Included in confirmed metrics (national macro rows kept separate from company totals).
Confidence Tiers Tier 2 Audited Corporate Disclosures: Listed-company quarterly headcount reports, earnings releases, and formal CEO restructuring announcements. Included in confirmed company KPIs.
Confidence Tiers Tier 3 Quarantined Leads & Estimates: Single-source media reports, unconfirmed ranges, or voluntary buyout eligibility pools (e.g., Microsoft VEP). Shown with dashed amber rings when enabled and never mixed into confirmed totals.
Confidence Tiers Tier 4 Unverified Noise: Anonymous social posts, forum rumors, or recycled blog summaries — excluded from the tracker entirely.
Country Grades Grade A 80–100 High Statutory Visibility: Mandatory advance layoff notices plus timely national payrolls make real workforce shifts highly auditable (e.g., United States at 84/100).
Country Grades Grade B 65–79 Solid Visibility with Moderate Gaps: Reliable national payrolls and redundancy filings exist, though mid-size firms, contractors, or remote teams leave partial blind spots (e.g., UK 76/100, Germany 75/100, Canada 74/100).
Country Grades Grade C 45–64 Macro Visible, Company-Level Opaque: Formal macro payrolls are published, but statutory thresholds, supervisory exclusions, silent exits, or regime breaks hide private white-collar cuts (e.g., India 52/100, Brazil 58/100).
Country Grades Grade D 0–44 Low Observability: Sparse or heavily lagged statutory reporting; public layoff and hiring claims cannot be verified independently.
Score Reading 52/100 vs 74/100 Practicality Score (0–100): Estimates how much of a country's real hiring and firing can be verified from public records. 52/100 (India) means macro formal payroll trends are trackable with adjustments, but roughly half of company-level white-collar movement stays internal. 74/100 (Canada) means nearly three-quarters is auditable via official filings.
Score Reading Obs / Noise Actual Obs vs. News Distortion (0–100): Left number = how much real activity appears in statutory data (higher is better). Right number = how heavily headlines, rumors, ghost job postings, or MoU pledges distort reality (higher is noisier). Example: India's 48 / 79 Firing score signals low statutory visibility paired with high media noise.

Frequently Asked Questions

How are confirmed tech layoffs and hiring numbers verified on this map?

Confirmed headline totals include only non-quarantined Tier 1 statutory filings (such as US BLS, State WARN notices, SEC Form 8-K, and SEBI disclosures) and Tier 2 audited corporate announcements. Single-source media estimates, voluntary buyout eligibility pools, and national macro payroll series are separated so they never distort company-level totals.

Why do public layoff trackers disagree with US BLS and India EPFO payroll data?

Media layoff trackers aggregate multi-quarter restructuring plans and global announcements, whereas statutory payroll surveys measure completed domestic separations after notice periods close. In India, raising the mandatory EPFO wage ceiling from Rs. 15,000 to Rs. 25,000 on September 17, 2026 also introduced a structural break that inflates raw gross additions unless newly mandated existing staff are separated from fresh hires.

How should I interpret Country Observability Grades (A–D) and Practicality Scores (0–100)?

Practicality Scores estimate how much real hiring and firing is verifiable through public statutory filings. Grade A (80–100, e.g. US at 84/100) indicates strong advance layoff and payroll visibility; Grade B (65–79, e.g. Canada at 74/100) reflects solid macro coverage with moderate blind spots; Grade C (45–64, e.g. India at 52/100) means formal macro payrolls exist but private white-collar exits largely stay internal; Grade D (0–44) indicates severe opacity.
