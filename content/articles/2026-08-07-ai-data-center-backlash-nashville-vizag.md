The local fight over AI's physical footprint went global this week. On Tuesday evening, Nashville's Metro Council voted 27-5 to let the city seize the site of a planned [$700 million data center](https://www.costar.com/article/970809918/nashville-council-approves-eminent-domain-action-to-halt-data-center-project) next to its zoo — using eminent domain. The same week, India's Andhra Pradesh High Court directed the state to answer a public-interest petition against Google's [$15 billion AI hub](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/08/06/googles-15-billion-india-data-centre-project-battles-water-wildlife-concerns) in Visakhapatnam, with a hearing set for August 24. Cities and courts, not chip supplies, are becoming the friction in AI's buildout.

## What Nashville actually voted for

The bill, BL2026-1489, passed its [third and final reading](https://www.nashvillescene.com/news/pithinthewind/metro-council-eminent-domain-zoo-data-center/article_6de94f8c-c3bb-5b41-a93a-d9e25c048817.html) on August 4. It authorizes Metro Nashville to acquire a roughly 23.5-acre parcel at 648 Grassmere Park — bought in July for [$23 million](https://www.wsmv.com/2026/07/17/dc-blox-closes-land-intended-data-center-next-nashville-zoo/) by Atlanta-based developer DC BLOX — by negotiation or condemnation. The claimed public use is not the zoo: it is relocated city offices, warehouse and training space, needed because an [FAA-approved runway expansion](https://fox17.com/news/local/nashville-mayor-bna-expansion-drives-eminent-domain-push-for-data-center-site) at Nashville's airport will displace an existing Metro campus — and office space, the city argues, satisfies the condemnation law's public-use test.

The price of stopping the project may be steep. Metro's own appraisal of the land is [$37.4 million](https://wpln.org/post/metro-advances-eminent-domain-effort-to-try-to-block-dc-blox-data-center/), the likely floor for "just compensation" — well above what DC BLOX paid weeks earlier. Not everyone was comfortable: council member Ginny Welsch called the move a ["gross misuse"](https://www.nashvillescene.com/news/pithinthewind/metro-council-eminent-domain-zoo-data-center/article_6de94f8c-c3bb-5b41-a93a-d9e25c048817.html) of condemnation power.

The emotion is real. Nashville Zoo says the facility — pitched at 10 megawatts initially, with the zoo warning of far higher figures — would put [constant cooling noise and light pollution](https://www.nashvillezoo.org/our-blog/posts/say-no-to-the-proposed-data-center) beside 3,000 animals, including a clouded-leopard breeding programme and the federally protected Nashville crayfish. A petition against the project has passed [500,000 signatures](https://wpln.org/post/metro-advances-eminent-domain-effort-to-try-to-block-dc-blox-data-center/). DC BLOX, which says its design addresses the noise concerns, said only that it had ["no updates to share"](https://www.costar.com/article/970809918/nashville-council-approves-eminent-domain-action-to-halt-data-center-project) after the vote; its attorney has said condemning land to interfere with a private purchase raises ["serious legal questions"](https://www.axios.com/local/nashville/2026/06/29/mayor-occonnell-eminent-domain-nashville-zoo-data-center) under Tennessee law. Such cases [typically take 12 to 18 months](https://www.wsmv.com/2026/08/05/nashville-zoo-data-center-what-comes-next-after-metro-councils-vote-eminent-domain/), so the blocking move starts a process rather than ending one. It also capped a deliberate three-step campaign: two weeks earlier the council unanimously passed a [data-center zoning code and a temporary permit moratorium](https://nashville.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=8040869&GUID=007B8704-62ED-46D1-9E9A-8B3F778229A8) — define, pause, then take.

What makes the mechanism striking is the inversion. Across the US, eminent domain has lately worked *for* data centers: Georgia Power is acquiring [about 30 residential properties](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/data-centers/30-georgia-homes-are-being-reclaimed-via-sale-or-eminent-domain-to-expand-power-grid-one-affected-family-member-says-its-for-the-data-centers) to expand grid capacity largely serving data centers. Nashville just pointed the same tool the other way.

## The India angle

India's version runs through courtrooms, and its stakes are water. The Andhra Pradesh High Court this week directed the state government to respond to a petition from Jal Biradari, a water-conservation group, against the [$15 billion Google–AdaniConneX–Airtel AI hub](https://www.freepressjournal.in/india/googles-15-billion-andhra-pradesh-data-centre-project-faces-water-wildlife-concerns-amid-legal-challenges) in Visakhapatnam, with the next hearing on August 24. The petitioners argue the gigawatt-scale campus would strain a city that already receives about [410 million litres of water a day against a 480-million-litre requirement](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/08/06/googles-15-billion-india-data-centre-project-battles-water-wildlife-concerns), and that one campus sits roughly 860 metres from the Kambalakonda Wildlife Sanctuary. It is one of several challenges the project has drawn this year: the Human Rights Forum has separately filed three cases at India's environmental court over water drawn from a rural drinking-water scheme.

Google, which [broke ground in April](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2026-04-28-Google-Breaks-Ground-on-India-AI-Hub,-Launching-a-National-Industrial-Ecosystem-Alongside-Indias-Digital-Infrastructure-Milestone) on the nearly one-gigawatt project, says it will use ["advanced air cooling to protect vital local water resources."](https://www.freepressjournal.in/india/googles-15-billion-andhra-pradesh-data-centre-project-faces-water-wildlife-concerns-amid-legal-challenges) The state calls the objections misleading; it has courted the project with [land discounts, power-tariff rebates and GST reimbursements](https://www.thenewsminute.com/andhra-pradesh/inside-the-subsidies-for-googles-vizag-data-centre-amid-andhra-karnataka-spar) that a rival state minister publicly questioned. And the buildout is not waiting: on August 6, Microsoft's fourth Indian cloud region [went live in Hyderabad](https://news.microsoft.com/source/asia/features/microsofts-newest-india-datacenter-region-goes-live-to-power-the-countrys-ai-economy-and-enable-frontier-firms/), advertising zero-water cooling, on the back of a cumulative $20.5 billion India commitment. India crossed [1,700 MW of data-centre capacity](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/story/india-data-centre-capacity-to-jump-30-in-2026-500-mw-supply-boost-expected-cbre-report-523529-2026-04-01) at the end of 2025, per CBRE, and is projected to add about 30% more in 2026 — while researchers project the sector's water use [more than doubling by 2030](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cgr417pwek7o) in one of the world's most water-stressed large economies. The tension Nashville expresses through zoning, India expresses through water.

## Why resistance is spiking now

Data Center Watch, the tracker most often cited, counted [$64 billion in US projects blocked or delayed](https://www.datacenterwatch.org/report) between May 2024 and March 2025 — then roughly [$130 billion in the first quarter of 2026 alone](https://www.datacenterwatch.org/q1-2026), across more than 75 projects, with opposition groups active in 49 states.

Three drivers explain it. First, scale: the IEA projects global data-centre electricity use roughly [doubling to about 950 TWh by 2030](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary), with AI-focused facilities tripling their consumption, and in the US it expects data centers to account for [about half of all electricity-demand growth to 2030](https://www.iea.org/reports/electricity-2026/demand). Second, bills: PJM's independent market monitor tied [$23 billion in customer price increases through 2028](https://fortune.com/2026/07/14/data-centers-23-billion-electricity-bills/) primarily to data-center demand in America's largest grid region; IEEFA estimates capacity-market effects alone add [$16-21 a month](https://ieefa.org/resources/projected-data-center-growth-spurs-pjm-capacity-prices-factor-10) to some household bills. Third, the money is unprecedented and visible: the four biggest hyperscalers guided to a combined [~$725 billion of 2026 capex](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/big-tech/big-techs-ai-spending-plans-reach-725-billion), up 77% in a year — the same buildout we traced in [the real cost of running AI](/articles/real-cost-of-running-ai/).

Public opinion has followed the bills. Gallup found [71% of Americans oppose an AI data center in their own area](https://news.gallup.com/poll/709772/americans-oppose-data-centers-area.aspx). Pew's read is more precise about why: [39% say data centers are mostly bad for the environment](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/03/12/how-americans-view-data-centers-impact-in-key-areas-from-the-environment-to-jobs/) and 38% for energy costs, against just 15% for local jobs — people believe the economic upside but doubt who pays the physical costs.

## What it means

The industry's answer is jobs and tax revenue; the honest reading: real, but smaller than advertised. An industry-commissioned PwC study claims data centers support [5.5 million US jobs](https://ppc.land/data-centers-5-5m-jobs-927bn-gdp-and-no-proof-they-raised-your-energy-bills/); Brookings' independent county-level analysis finds hyperscale facilities do lift local employment [4-5% over five to six years](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/new-evidence-on-data-center-employment-effects/) — thousands of jobs in a typical county, not tens of thousands — and concludes industry estimates overstate the effect by about a factor of three.

Data centers have also become electoral: Virginia's 2025 governor's race, in the state with the [world's largest data-center concentration](https://www.eenews.net/articles/with-data-center-fights-tearing-apart-towns-virginians-cast-ballots/), put the issue on the debate stage. Washington pulls the other way: a July 2025 executive order [fast-tracks federal permitting](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/07/accelerating-federal-permitting-of-data-center-infrastructure/) of large AI facilities, and the president attacked New York's permit pause [within a day](https://www.eenews.net/articles/trump-knocks-hochuls-data-center-moratorium/), calling data centers "LIQUID GOLD." For AI companies, siting risk now belongs on the same planning sheet as chips and power-purchase agreements: the constraint on the [price war among frontier models](/articles/gpt-5-6-luna-price-cut/) may ultimately be a county commission, a high court, or a zoo. For communities, Nashville and Vizag are templates — one fiscal, one judicial — and unlike [Europe's rules-first approach](/articles/eu-ai-act-august-2026-what-changed/), the US and India are writing their AI-infrastructure law one flashpoint at a time.

## What to watch

Whether DC BLOX sues: no independent legal scholar has yet publicly assessed whether Nashville's "public use" would survive a pretextual-taking challenge. The August 24 hearing in Andhra Pradesh, and whether Vizag's construction pace changes before it. New York's statewide environmental study, which will define what a "moratorium with an off-ramp" looks like. And the money: if hyperscaler capex keeps compounding while the blocked-project tally grows in step, expect the industry to shift from fighting sites one by one to pushing state-level permitting reform — the quiet battle we track across [our Policy & Society coverage](/topics/policy/).
