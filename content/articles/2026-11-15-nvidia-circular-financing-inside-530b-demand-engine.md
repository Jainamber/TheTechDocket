Following the sudden resignation of CEO Jensen Huang, reports allege that NVIDIA Corp. funded AI startups to buy its own GPUs to sustain demand. According to [reports from Lavx](https://news.lavx.hu/article/report-alleges-nvidia-funded-ai-startups-to-buy-gpus-and-sustain-demand), this strategy of circular financing allowed the chipmaker to maintain high GPU demand and keep hardware pricing artificially elevated.

## The Mechanics of NVIDIA's Circular Financing

NVIDIA expanded its total outstanding commitments and guarantees to [$530.5 billion](https://dev.to/frankchu/530b-in-guarantees-nvidia-funded-its-own-demand-4g89) to prop up demand for its GPUs. This strategy involved funding smaller cloud providers and AI startups, who immediately used those funds to purchase NVIDIA hardware, routing the cash back as immediate revenue.

To understand this system, we must examine the balance sheet. In its quarterly disclosures, the company revealed a massive accumulation of financial exposure. The total commitments and guarantees ballooned from [$184 billion](https://dev.to/frankchu/530b-in-guarantees-nvidia-funded-its-own-demand-4g89) to [$530.5 billion](https://dev.to/frankchu/530b-in-guarantees-nvidia-funded-its-own-demand-4g89) in a single quarter, reflecting a concerted effort to underwrite the artificial intelligence infrastructure market. A substantial portion of this, specifically [$279 billion](https://dev.to/frankchu/530b-in-guarantees-nvidia-funded-its-own-demand-4g89), represents direct supply commitments for memory and chip wafers, while another [$36 billion](https://dev.to/frankchu/530b-in-guarantees-nvidia-funded-its-own-demand-4g89) is allocated to buy back unused cloud capacity. This circular loop is detailed in reports from outlets like [TechPowerUp](https://www.techpowerup.com/354814/nvidia-allegedly-engineered-scarcity-by-funding-ai-startups-to-buy-its-gpus), which highlight how these mechanisms kept hardware prices artificially high.

The financial strain of this strategy is evident in the company's accounts receivable, which climbed to [$63.1 billion](https://dev.to/frankchu/530b-in-guarantees-nvidia-funded-its-own-demand-4g89), representing a sequential [64% increase](https://dev.to/frankchu/530b-in-guarantees-nvidia-funded-its-own-demand-4g89) from the previous quarter's [$38 billion](https://dev.to/frankchu/530b-in-guarantees-nvidia-funded-its-own-demand-4g89). Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) jumped from a historical average of [45 days](https://dev.to/frankchu/530b-in-guarantees-nvidia-funded-its-own-demand-4g89) to [60 days](https://dev.to/frankchu/530b-in-guarantees-nvidia-funded-its-own-demand-4g89) in a single quarter as the chipmaker extended payment terms from the standard Net 30/45 to over [90 days](https://dev.to/frankchu/530b-in-guarantees-nvidia-funded-its-own-demand-4g89) for selected customers. According to [financial analysis reports](https://dev.to/frankchu/530b-in-guarantees-nvidia-funded-its-own-demand-4g89), NVIDIA's direct equity stakes in private AI startups and neoclouds also jumped from [$22 billion](https://dev.to/frankchu/530b-in-guarantees-nvidia-funded-its-own-demand-4g89) to [$48 billion](https://dev.to/frankchu/530b-in-guarantees-nvidia-funded-its-own-demand-4g89) since January 2026. This aggressive financing campaign came as major hyperscalers accelerated their transition to custom, in-house silicon, threatening to leave NVIDIA with an avalanche of pre-paid TSMC wafers. We previously analyzed the initial wave of these claims in our coverage of the [Nvidia scarcity allegations](/articles/nvidia-scarcity-allegations-did-chipmaker-fund-own-sales/).

## The OpenAI Ohio Deal and the Boardroom Rift

According to [NVIDIA's SEC Form 8-K filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000069/nvda-20260817.htm), the company entered into residual-value guarantees with SB Energy for leases of approximately 4.25 gigawatts of IT load, with an aggregate payment obligation capped at $105 billion. This massive agreement became a primary focal point in the subsequent transition of leadership to interim CEO Colette Kress.

The tension inside NVIDIA reached a boiling point during an August 2026 finance meeting. According to reports published by [The Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/international-business/deer-in-the-headlights-colette-kress-went-from-clashing-with-huang-to-leading-nvidia/articleshow/135812004.cms), Huang cut off Kress mid-presentation, calling her a reporting CFO who was acting like a deer in the headlights when she urged caution regarding the company's ballooning credit guarantees. Kress was highly concerned about a proposed [$250 billion](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/international-business/deer-in-the-headlights-colette-kress-went-from-clashing-with-huang-to-leading-nvidia/articleshow/135812004.cms) financial backstop for OpenAI's data center campus, successfully fighting to scale back the signed guarantee cap to a maximum of [$120 billion](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/international-business/deer-in-the-headlights-colette-kress-went-from-clashing-with-huang-to-leading-nvidia/articleshow/135812004.cms).

The finalized transaction is documented in NVIDIA's [SEC Form 8-K filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000069/nvda-20260817.htm), which outlines the residual-value guarantees for up to [4.25 gigawatts](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000069/nvda-20260817.htm) of IT load developed by SoftBank's SB Energy subsidiary. Under this agreement, NVIDIA's aggregate payment obligation is capped at [$105 billion](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000069/nvda-20260817.htm). This complex transaction represents a massive credit risk, as OpenAI lacks an investment-grade credit rating. We previously explored the long-term implications of this agreement in our detailed analysis of [Nvidia's $105B OpenAI deal](/articles/nvidia-openai-105b-ohio-deal/).

Adding fuel to the internal tensions was the rapid promotion of Nico Caprez to vice president of global AI infrastructure growth. Senior finance personnel questioned the highly leveraged financing models being advanced within these divisions, which concentrated significant default risk onto NVIDIA's balance sheet.

## The India Angle

Indian technology startups and enterprise customers face a severe supply squeeze and high local markups as NVIDIA prioritizes high-margin global data centers. This has forced local firms to navigate a steep [18% goods and services tax](https://www.rvvisualcraft.shop/blog/nvidia-controversy-2026-ai-monopoly-gpu-pricing-indian-creators) alongside premium retail pricing on consumer GPUs.

The global reallocation of 3-nanometer wafer capacity toward lucrative enterprise AI accelerators has left Indian creators and local AI builders facing severe hardware shortages. According to market analyses by [RV Visual Craft](https://www.rvvisualcraft.shop/blog/nvidia-controversy-2026-ai-monopoly-gpu-pricing-indian-creators), the retail price of consumer graphics cards in India has been artificially inflated due to limited local stock allocations. For instance, a high-end consumer GPU with a US MSRP of [$1,999](https://www.rvvisualcraft.shop/blog/nvidia-controversy-2026-ai-monopoly-gpu-pricing-indian-creators) converts to approximately [₹1,68,915](https://www.rvvisualcraft.shop/blog/nvidia-controversy-2026-ai-monopoly-gpu-pricing-indian-creators) at an exchange rate of [₹84.50 per USD](https://www.rvvisualcraft.shop/blog/nvidia-controversy-2026-ai-monopoly-gpu-pricing-indian-creators). While the Basic Customs Duty (BCD) on IT hardware under the ITA-1 agreement is [0%](https://www.rvvisualcraft.shop/blog/nvidia-controversy-2026-ai-monopoly-gpu-pricing-indian-creators), the addition of an [18% Integrated Goods and Services Tax (IGST)](https://www.rvvisualcraft.shop/blog/nvidia-controversy-2026-ai-monopoly-gpu-pricing-indian-creators) of [₹30,405](https://www.rvvisualcraft.shop/blog/nvidia-controversy-2026-ai-monopoly-gpu-pricing-indian-creators) brings the true landed cost to [₹1,99,320](https://www.rvvisualcraft.shop/blog/nvidia-controversy-2026-ai-monopoly-gpu-pricing-indian-creators). However, actual retail prices in the Indian grey market often exceed [₹2,40,000](https://www.rvvisualcraft.shop/blog/nvidia-controversy-2026-ai-monopoly-gpu-pricing-indian-creators), exposing a steep brand premium of more than [20%](https://www.rvvisualcraft.shop/blog/nvidia-controversy-2026-ai-monopoly-gpu-pricing-indian-creators) that local buyers must pay.

This supply constraint is particularly challenging for small-scale Indian AI operations trying to deploy local models. While the Indian government's IndiaAI Mission has set aside funding to establish local GPU capacity, the immediate shortage of physical hardware forces developers to rely on expensive global cloud APIs. These cloud expenses are further compounded by an [18% IGST](https://www.rvvisualcraft.shop/blog/nvidia-controversy-2026-ai-monopoly-gpu-pricing-indian-creators) on import of services and a standard [3.5% bank forex markup](https://www.rvvisualcraft.shop/blog/nvidia-controversy-2026-ai-monopoly-gpu-pricing-indian-creators) on international credit card transactions. A monthly API invoice of [$1,000](https://www.rvvisualcraft.shop/blog/nvidia-controversy-2026-ai-monopoly-gpu-pricing-indian-creators), which should nominally cost [₹84,500](https://www.rvvisualcraft.shop/blog/nvidia-controversy-2026-ai-monopoly-gpu-pricing-indian-creators) ultimately costs an Indian startup [₹102,667](https://www.rvvisualcraft.shop/blog/nvidia-controversy-2026-ai-monopoly-gpu-pricing-indian-creators) after factoring in these regulatory surcharges. These financial hurdles are encouraging local firms to look toward open-source models as an alternative, a trend we highlighted when [OpenAI slowed down AI training](/articles/openai-slows-ai-training/) to focus on cost optimization. ## What to Watch

The transition of leadership to interim CEO Colette Kress marks a critical shift toward financial conservatism and balance-sheet cleanup at NVIDIA. Regulatory scrutiny from global antitrust bodies will determine whether the company's circular financing practices constitute market manipulation.

With General Counsel Tim Teter taking control of all corporate communications, NVIDIA is entering a period of intense damage control. Kress's primary challenge will be unwinding the complex web of credit guarantees without triggering defaults among the highly leveraged neoclouds that rely on NVIDIA's financial backing. Industry analysts are closely watching whether the company will be forced to write down a portion of its [$48 billion](https://dev.to/frankchu/530b-in-guarantees-nvidia-funded-its-own-demand-4g89) in private equity stakes or face severe write-offs if any of its major funded partners default on their GPU leases.

Furthermore, the broader AI industry is bracing for a potential correction in hardware demand. If neoclouds and startups stop receiving credit-backed GPU shipments, the true market demand for AI chips will finally be exposed. This transition could lead to a sudden oversupply of silicon, putting downward pressure on GPU pricing and significantly impacting NVIDIA's high GAAP gross margin, which stood at [75.0%](https://dev.to/frankchu/530b-in-guarantees-nvidia-funded-its-own-demand-4g89) in the last quarterly report. As the company navigates this transition, the tech sector will finally learn whether the massive demand of the past [three years](https://dev.to/frankchu/530b-in-guarantees-nvidia-funded-its-own-demand-4g89) was a genuine economic engine or a beautifully engineered financial loop.
